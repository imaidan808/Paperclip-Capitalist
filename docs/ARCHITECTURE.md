## Engine contract
`new Game({perks: [a, b], specialty, handle, pid, startHour, seed})`, where `perks` are the ids of exactly two of the twelve Backgrounds in `js/data/perks.js` (an unknown id is dropped, so the game would start without it), then `game.advanceTo(hour)` and the action methods (`pitch`, `buyLot`, `repair`, `sellPool`, `ship`, `hirePipeline`, `draw`, `repay`, `resolveAudit`, `exit`, `createOffer`/`acceptOffer`/`finalizeOffer`). Every action returns `{ok, msg, ...}`. State is a plain JSON object (`game.s`); the RNG seed is part of it, so a save replays identically. The environment (`game.env`) injects the cohort, difficulty and world seed. Quests, achievements, daily bounties and the Codex live in `game.s.meta` and are scored by `Progress` (`js/meta/progress.js`) from the events the engine emits (`game.onEvent`).

## The ledger
`game.s.ledger` is a **gain-and-cost ledger, not a cash book**, on purpose: a row is what an action gained or cost against fair value, and some rows are not cash. A sale's row is the gain over the crate's fair value (received minus fair value), a pool sale is booked whole when it is made (the cash lands after the delay), a classmate swap is the difference in value between what came in and what went out, and a manager's principal in flight has no row until the cycle ends; every other cash movement has its own row (buy, repair, fees, loans, repayments, fines, rent). So cash minus the sum of the rows is not zero, and it is not meant to be: the Bank drawer says so under the ledger ("a line is a gain or a cost, not always a cash payment"), the full rules are in the comment above `Game.led`, and the Debrief reads the rows as evidence of what the student did. A reconciling cash statement is not built; if students need one it is a separate view over the same state (cash movements only), not a change to these rows.

## Room protocol (JSON over either transport)
| Message | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `hello`, `bye` | anyone | presence |
| `snap` | student | public snapshot: net worth, skills, 48h niche counts, shock log, public inventory. What it says is believed only within bounds: the score is recomputed, an online founder is placed at the class's hour (within two days), and net worth is cut to what the best honest run reaches on that day (`nwCeiling`, `js/cohort.js`: $100 on day 1, $1.2M on day 30, $40M on day 90), so a made-up fortune is no better than the best real one could be by then and no better; the server tally below is the real fix |
| `clock` | instructor | `{hour, speed, paused, difficulty, ghosts, algo}`; students adopt only the instructor's (or, with no instructor, the lowest Founder ID's) |
| `inject`, `rewind` | instructor | deploy a shock (idempotent by id) / restore daily checkpoints. Both are measured from the instructor's clock, so `Session.inject` and `Session.rewind` refuse (`{ ok: false, msg }`, nothing sent, no throw) while a room taken back with its recovery code is still waiting to hear the class's hour (`recovering`): its own clock reads hour 0, and a rewind from there would send the whole class to Day 1. The dashboard holds the same controls; the session no longer relies on it |
| `offer`, `accept`, `confirm`, `deliver`, `ack`, `decline`, `withdraw`, `recall`, `holding`, `free` | student | peer swap in three steps: the proposer escrows and makes the offer; the addressee asks to `accept`; the proposer `confirm`s that it still holds the escrow (and holds it for them: it cannot be withdrawn or expire under them, once per try and twice per offer); only then does the addressee commit (and not at all on a confirmation that comes after its own 30-second wait) and `deliver`. The addressee has committed when it delivers, so the proposer takes any reply that names the crates it asked for (by id and class) and the exact cash, whatever district a crate is in now, credited as it described each crate and no better; only a reply that crates and cash together are worth under 45% of the description is refused. It answers `ack`, and the addressee sends the delivery again (five quick tries over 80 seconds, then every 30 seconds for half an hour; a committed swap is kept in the save, so a reload does not lose it) until it hears one. The proposer's hold on its escrow ends with a question and not a guess: when the hold has run 155 seconds (`LOCK_MS`: the acceptor's five quick tries run to 5 s x 5 x 5 = 125 seconds, and a confirmation can take the acceptor's own 30-second wait to arrive) it sends `recall`, and an addressee that has committed answers `holding` (and sends the delivery again at once, which renews the hold for five minutes, six times at most) while one that has not answers `free` (and forgets the offer, so a late confirmation cannot commit); only a `free`, 60 seconds of silence (`RECALL_WAIT_MS`) or a seventh lapse lets the owner withdraw or the engine expire the offer. A delivery lost for any time up to the half hour is therefore taken, and nothing is made twice. A swap whose addressee was confirmed is finished even if the bell rang meanwhile |
| `flag` | student | a blocked offer outside +/-40% of fair value |
| `royalty` | student | daily payment on a royalty stream. The payer's day's rate falls due for each day of the stream and is paid from the cash there is, in at most twelve messages a day (`ROYALTY_MSGS`), each at most a day's rate; what cash cannot cover stays owed as arrears (a liability in its net worth and in an exit's proceeds) and is paid first from the next cash. A stream whose receiver's run is over, or who is selling the business, ends. The receiver takes at most a day's rate a message and never more than the stream's cap (the least of what was promised in all and the worth it was counted at plus the 40% band), and never faster than the days go by: by the class's clock, a day's rate for each full day the stream has run, plus the day in progress and one in hand for a clock that runs a little behind, so a payer cannot send the whole of it at once. A promised royalty counts for at most a tenth of its promiser's net worth (`ROYALTY_NW`), and one a day larger than the promiser's whole worth is declined |

## Who is who on the relay
A Founder ID is a name, not a proof: every message carries its sender's, so every classmate knows everyone's, the instructor's included. So a connection to the relay must bring `key`, a random secret its browser keeps beside the ID (`pc.key.<slot>` in localStorage). The first connection to use an ID in a room binds the ID to a hash of its key (nothing else of it is kept, and it is never logged); a later connection for that ID with another key, or none, is refused. That is what stops a classmate from taking the instructor's seat, or another student's name and snapshot, by connecting with an ID they have read off the wire. The instructor's seat is the first connection to claim it, kept for its ID (and so its key), for as long as the room lives: another ID is never handed it by waiting (a laptop lid closed for a break must not make a student the instructor), and the first holder takes it back from anyone. An operator who wants a handover after a long absence sets `SEAT_GRACE_MS` (milliseconds; 0, never, by default). The other way to the seat is the room's **owner token**: a random secret (32 hex characters) the instructor's browser makes when the room is opened and keeps beside the room's code. It is sent as `owner` in the connection, never to a student, and the relay keeps only a hash of it (`sha256` of the room code, `owner` and the token), as it keeps only a hash of a Founder ID's key. The first instructor to bring a token binds the room to it, whoever sits in the seat then (a holder that showed no token is no owner: a stray tab, or the first to arrive after a restart, does not leave the room without one for good); from then on whoever brings the same token is the owner, from any device and under any Founder ID, and takes the seat from whoever sits in it. A token that does not match the bound one changes nothing, and a connection with none claims the seat as before. The **recovery code** the instructor is shown is this token in groups of four: it is what "Take back a room" asks for, with the room code. A recovered instructor does not know the class's hour, so it waits 15 seconds for a student's clock and otherwise starts paused at the hour the class's snapshots show (`RECOVER_WAIT_MS`). A connection that closes is announced to the room as a `bye`, and a pid that has been gone 30 seconds (`GHOST_MS`) is forgotten when the table of pids is full, the names that only greeted before those that sent a snapshot, so a script that greets or snapshots and leaves cannot fill it for good. A full relay forgets the room that has been empty longest before it turns a class away; with no room empty it forgets the quietest one, if nobody in it has sent anything the relay kept for `IDLE_MS` (5 minutes), and a full room drops the connection that has been silent longest (also at least `IDLE_MS`) and never the instructor's seat, so a script that opens sockets and sends nothing cannot keep a class out. (A script that keeps its sockets in use is not stopped by this: that needs a limit per address, which is the operator's, behind a campus NAT or a proxy.) The client does not retry a close with code 1008, which is the relay refusing this connection (a Founder ID that another connection bound first in this room, or a bad room code): the room is `refused`, the player is told in words, and what to do is to ask for a new room. Two tabs of one browser with the same slot share both. Over a browser's own tabs (`BroadcastChannel`) there is no relay and no key: every tab is the same browser, and it is not a place to defend against.

## A tab is a founder
A run is kept in `localStorage` as `pc.<name>.<slot>`. Until this round there was one slot for a page address: `main`, or the one `?slot=name` named. So every tab of a browser was one founder, and the room between the tabs of one browser (BroadcastChannel, no relay) that the dashboard, the title screen and the faculty kit advertise could not work: a student tab opened with the dashboard's plain **Copy student link** (`origin + path + ?room=CODE`) read the instructor's Founder ID from `pc.pid.main`, `Session._onMsg` drops every message whose sender is its own ID, so the instructor counted nobody (`0 students online`), and a second student tab was offered the first one's saved run to replace (`pc.save.main`) and wrote over it from then on. Measured in headless Chrome, three tabs of one profile (instructor, student A, student B), before the change: one Founder ID (`pc.pid.main`) and one relay key (`pc.key.main`) for all three, one saved run (`pc.save.main`, B's, after A's was replaced), one remembered alias and one set of daily checkpoints (`pc.handle.main`, `pc.ckdays.main`, `pc.ck.N.main`); the Debrief's notes (`pc.reflection.main`) are shared by the same rule. The instructor listed no peer and no student.

What changed, in `js/identity.js` (pure; the storages are handed in, so a Node test can open any number of tabs of one browser):
- **Two keyspaces.** Which names belong to a founder is one list (`isFounderName`): the Founder ID (`pid`), the relay key (`key`), the alias (`handle`), the saved run (`save`), its daily checkpoints (`ckdays`, `ck.N`) and the debrief notes (`reflection`). Everything else is the browser's and stays in `main` (or the slot the address names): the settings, the Academy's progress and rewards, the Codex, the relay address, an instructor's room, its owner token (recovery code) and its roster.
- **A student tab becomes a founder of its own when it begins in a room with no relay** (`Session.takeTabIdentity`, called from the draft's Begin in `js/main.js`, before the saved run is looked at and before the room is joined): a new slot `t-xxxxxxxx` is made, its name is written to the tab's own `sessionStorage` (`pc.tab`), and the Founder ID, key and alias are read from it (made on first use). A refresh of the tab finds `pc.tab` and is the same student, resumed with Resume your run; a new tab has no `pc.tab` and is a new student. The draft starts with no alias in such a tab (`Session.draftHandle`), not the last one another founder of this browser typed.
- **What does not change.** A page opened with `?slot=name` keeps every name in that slot and takes no tab slot (the person chose). A room with a relay keeps the browser's ID (the relay binds an ID to its browser's key; the same student in a new tab must still be that student). A solo player's `pc.*.main` keys are never read or written by a student tab; an old save is byte for byte what it was and loads as before (`tests/class-tabs.test.mjs`). The instructor is the browser's founder, in `main`, and keeps its owner token and its roster there.
- **A copy of a tab is not the same student.** Duplicate tab hands the copy the original's `sessionStorage`. A tab that is a founder of its own keeps a claim on its slot fresh (`pc.claim.<slot>`, touched every 2 s by the page's heartbeat, marked gone on `pagehide`); a tab that finds its slot claimed by another page that is alive within 6 s forgets the slot and is a new tab. Refresh is the page going away (`pagehide`) and the same tab loading again, so the claim is let go and the new page takes it back. A tab whose timers were throttled for minutes in the background can look dead for that long; the worst that costs is that a copy of it is not told from it, which is how every tab behaved before.
- **Tidying.** Slots of tabs not seen for 30 days, and any beyond the newest 40, are removed with everything in them when a tab takes a slot (`sweepTabs`): the slots of closed tabs would otherwise stay in `localStorage` (a run and its checkpoints are tens of KB) for ever. Only slots this module made, and only when they hold a claim, are ever removed; `main` and a slot a person named are not.
- **What the instructor sees.** Students are rows keyed by Founder ID, so two student tabs are two rows with their own names, the roster, the gradebook and its CSV, the cohort and the saved roster each hold both, and a student who refreshes is one row (same ID), online again once its snapshot arrives. A student who closes the tab and opens the link in a new one is a new student; the old row stays as away, as a student who changed browsers would.

## What a classmate's offer is checked against
A swap offer arrives from a classmate's browser, so what it says is checked against what that classmate has published to the room, before it is shown to anyone (`Session._publishedCovers`, `_onOffer`):
- **The offer against the published stock.** Every crate an offer describes must be in the stock the sender last published in its snapshot, with the same id, class and condition. Together the crates cannot be worth more than `(max(0, published net worth) + published debt) x 1.5 + 5`, where the published net worth is first cut to the best a run could have on that day (`nwCeiling`) and the debt is counted because the crates are bought with it. An offer that fails is answered with `decline`, so an honest proposer's escrow comes back. This is a bound and not a proof: the stock is the sender's own word, so a made-up crate is something the whole room can see in the roster and is bounded by the worth the sender can claim, not something the receiver can prove false.
- **A royalty against the promiser's worth.** What a promised royalty is worth is the promiser's published net worth, never the one written in the offer, and a royalty a day larger than that whole worth (and than $1) is declined.
- **The fair-value band.** Before either side commits, `checkOffer` compares what goes out with what comes in at the fair value each side can see, and blocks a swap outside +/-40% (`FMV_BAND`).

## Open decisions
Questions the engine must not decide silently. Each says what the build does now and what is left for the author to choose.

**One save slot: Begin replaces a saved run.** A run is saved in one place, `pc.save.<slot>` in the browser's storage (`kv` 'save', `js/session.js`), and `Session.startGame` writes it. When a run is saved the title screen offers Resume your run, New solo run and Join a classroom; the last two both open the Background draft, and the draft's Begin starts a new run, so it writes over the saved one. It used to do that without a word, and "Join a classroom" (a room code and one button) gave no hint that a saved solo run would be gone after Begin. Built, because it is small and only adds a step before an overwrite: Begin asks first (`replaceRunHTML` in `js/ui/draft.js`, `askReplace` in `js/main.js`). The dialog names the saved run (alias, day, Backgrounds, and the room if it was a classroom's: `describeSave` in `js/session.js`) and the run about to begin; "Keep my saved run" has the cursor and leaves the draft as it was (`onBegin` answers `false`, so the form's Begin works again and the picks are kept); only "Replace it and begin" writes. A save that `restore()` would refuse (the three-profile game's) is replaced without asking, because nothing playable is lost. What is still the author's to choose:
1. Keep it as it is: replace, after the question.
2. Keep both: park the solo run under a second key when a student joins a room and give it back when they leave. An instructor already works this way (`startInstructor` in `js/main.js` puts the solo save back after `S.reset()`). It needs a rule for the sentence "Left the room. You keep your solo run." (today it means the room's run carries on as a solo run) and for a reload inside a room, which restores the room's run from the one slot.
3. Refuse Join while a run is saved, until the player has resumed it or deleted it.
Related, left alone: the in-game menu's Join a room says "Joining restarts your run on the instructor's shared clock", but `onJoin` calls `S.reset()` at once, which deletes the save before the draft is drawn, so Back from that draft lands on a title with no Resume. It is the same choice (2 or 3 would change it too).

## Moving to a scalable, authoritative backend
1. Run `engine.js` on the server per student (it has no DOM), accept actions over HTTP/WebSocket, and persist `game.s` per Founder ID in Postgres with point-in-time recovery.
2. Move the cohort aggregation (`cohort.js`) server-side so niche volumes cannot be spoofed.
3. Validate peer trades server-side with the same `checkOffer`. Without it a client is still believed about one thing: the cash and the crates it says it handed over (the proposer credits what the reply says, clamped to the offer, but cannot check the other side really gave them up), so a hostile classmate can still take what a proposer escrowed for a crate it never parted with. Two sides' reports of the same swap, cross-checked, are the cheapest fix.
   The same goes for the other direction, the crate a proposer says it is giving. The client keeps a bound, not a proof: an offer is shown only for crates the sender has listed in its own published stock (same id, class and condition) and worth no more than 1.5 times its published net worth (cut to the best run of that day) plus its debt, so a made-up crate is something the whole room can see in the roster, and bounded by the worth the sender claims. A client-side "two-sided" check was considered and not built (the repair round of the v2 audit): the proposer's escrow record would travel with the offer, but it is the proposer's own word again, so a colluding pair proves nothing to each other's engine. Only the server tally proves it.
4. Add an LTI 1.3 launch to mint the pseudonymous Founder ID and an Assignment and Grade Services call from the gradebook export.
5. Keep the front end unchanged: swap `net.js`'s relay for the API.

## The 2.5D world
`World` (js/world/world.js) is a Canvas 2D renderer with no dependencies. The island is a tile grid in isometric projection (`sx = (gx - gy) * 32`, `sy = (gx + gy) * 16 - gz`); the sixteen districts sit on plateaus at different elevations joined by roads, bridges and a ferry. Each game tick `sync()` reconciles *entities* with live game state: buyer NPCs per lead (they walk to a slot and walk away when the lead goes), pallets per lot, crates per stored asset, buildings per pipeline (height = level), empty plots, trucks per freight run. Static scenery is a deterministic list in `layout.js`. The frame loop draws ground, then depth-sorted props and entities, then screen-space passes (night multiply, lights, rain, labels, bubbles). Hit shapes are recorded while drawing and picked back-to-front.

Rules that keep it honest: the world never changes game state itself (clicks go through `api.activate` to the same `acts` as the dashboard); one broken object cannot blank the scene (each item draws inside try/catch and logs once); the clock-driven day/night is rate-limited and fixed at fast clock speeds (no strobing); reduced motion draws only on change; quality drops automatically (30 to 20 to 15 fps, lower device-pixel ratio) on a slow machine. `tests/world.test.mjs` draws every entity type, district, mode, hour and shock against a recording canvas.

### Presentation notes (the polish pass)
These are drawing and layout rules only; none of them reads or changes game state.
- **Pixel density.** The canvas is drawn at the screen's density to 2x. Where the 2D canvas is on the CPU (a browser with the GPU off or emulated: it refuses a WebGL context asked for with `failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat`, see `softwareRaster` in `gfx.js`) the cap is 1.5x, because every full-window layer is a pass over all of its pixels on the main thread. The quality governor takes a further quarter off at its fourth tier and gives it back below its second, so a picture on the edge never reallocates the canvas back and forth.
- **The governor does not judge a load.** A frame that filled a cache (a ground chunk baked, a sprite or an atlas entry made, a cloud chunk baked: `builds` in `js/world/gfx.js`) paid a cost that is not repeated, and the frame after it arrives late because of it, so `World.govern` judges neither (`BUILD_GRACE` in `js/world/governor.js`: a build that goes on for 300 frames in a row, ten seconds of a still picture, is not a load and is judged as any frame is). Judged, the first seconds of a world sent a laptop four times slower than a Mac down five tiers and back, over and over: tier 3 turns the lights, smoke and people (`lite`) off, tier 4 takes the canvas density from 2 to 1.5 (a reallocation that bakes the whole ground again, which is more slow frames, which sends it down again), so the picture blinked: 13 changes in 14 seconds with the CPU slowed 4x, one now (`tests/world-crisp.test.mjs`, `tests/world.test.mjs`).
- **Light layers.** `Lighting` makes at most one full-size layer a frame (`perFrame`), so the first dusk spreads its night, crossfade and shafts over three frames instead of one 70 ms frame; a frame without its crossfade stamps the day's air alone. The shafts are baked into that crossfade (`Lighting.mix`, eight steps of their strength) and are never stamped on their own: on a CPU-drawn canvas a plain full-window stamp costs 0.9 to 1.6 ms and a stamp or fill in the additive or multiply composites 3.0 to 3.6, so a dusk frame is one plain stamp, the grade's one multiply tint and the light pools, and `World.draw()` at dusk is about 1 ms where it was 17 (the raster of the additive stamp ran inside it).
- **Sprites.** A zoom that is moving shows the prop's sprite of the zoom before, scaled; a sprite of more than 2.2 Mpx is built only once the scale has rested; the memory budget grows with the density. `World.detach()` gives every bitmap back (`release()`).
- **The camera buttons.** They stand over one edge of the window, so `fit()`, the camera clamp, the district name tags and the map's cards use the part they leave free (`World.inset`, measured by `worldview.js` from the real buttons).
- **Zooming the map.** The scroll wheel, a trackpad's two-finger scroll and a trackpad pinch all zoom the map about the pointer; the camera buttons and `+` / `-` keep their steps (`0` fits the island). One non-passive listener on the canvas cancels every wheel event that is aimed at it, and Safari, which sends a pinch as `gesturestart`, `gesturechange` and `gestureend` instead of a wheel, has the same three listeners. The canvas has no children and the HUD, drawers and dialogs sit above it as siblings, so their wheel never reaches it (the page does not scroll, zoom or navigate from a gesture on the map, and a pinch that starts on the HUD is the browser's own page zoom, left alone on purpose: it is how a low-vision visitor makes the page bigger); the browser test in `tests/camera-input.test.mjs` holds that structure. `js/world/camera-input.js` is pure and holds the rules. The source of a wheel event is told from the event itself: a mouse notch is deltaMode 1, or a whole deltaY of 50 or more with no deltaX, or (on a Mac) a deltaY that is a whole multiple of 4.000244 and no whole number itself; a fractional number of lines with Ctrl is a pinch; Ctrl set on anything else is a pinch; the rest is a scroll. A notch is one step of 1.16 that eases in at the pointer (a time constant of 90 ms, driven by the world's own loop and only while an ease is pending); a pinch follows the fingers 1:1 and is applied at once; a scroll is gentle (0.001 a pixel) and is applied at once, and each *gesture* of it (events less than 100 ms apart, so the momentum after the fingers lift is part of it) has a budget of about four times the zoom, its gain tapering as the budget goes, so one hard flick ends softly and the range takes a few deliberate strokes. No one event moves the zoom by more than 1.6 times. A camera glide of the world's own (Fit, Show me, a district change) is not broken by a scroll or the rest of its momentum; a pinch or a notch still takes over. Under reduced motion every notch zooms at once. A drag, a camera button, a key and every move of the camera's own (`goTo`) end an ease on its way (a button or key pressed during one steps on from where the notch was going). Every kind of zoom ends in `World.zoomAbout`, which also takes hold of the ground again for a pointer that is panning (`rebaseDrag`), so a zoom in the middle of a drag, or the momentum of the last swipe, does not slide the grabbed ground off the pointer. The hover is looked at again, once a drawn frame and after the drawing, whenever the camera moved under a mouse that did not (`repickHover`: not for a finger, a mouse that left the canvas or while the ground is held). `tests/camera-input.test.mjs`.
- **Dialogs and the Academy card.** A closed `<dialog>` is never drawn (`dialog:not([open]) { display: none }`, and `dlg` takes its class off when it closes), and when the control that opened it is gone the cursor goes to the open drawer's title, else to the game. The Deal Room's entrance animation fills `backwards`, never `both`: a held `transform: none` makes the sheet the containing block of every fixed box inside it, the coach layer among them. The coach card follows into any open window (a step about a buyer or lot in the world is shown there with no ring), and the layer is marked `data-keep` so `setHTML` leaves it where it is when the sheet repaints. Where the card stands, which shape it has and when it is a tab is one rule, set out in "The coach card covers nothing" below. While a press is in flight the card does not move, change shape or change its words (`pressing` in `js/ui/academy.js`): pressing a control moves the cursor to it, the cursor is the first thing the card keeps off, and a card that hops over the control being pressed takes the release; what was owed is painted at the release, after its click. A click closes a dialog only if the press began on the dialog itself (`initDialog`, `js/ui/common.js`), so a press that ends on something laid over the dialog closes nothing. A toast the card stands over is let go dim (`toastsYield`, `.toast.ac-under`; never one that says an action failed), because toasts stand above the card and come at the very step they belong to. The card's buttons stick to its foot while its words scroll above them (`.ac-btns`). The place a card the player opened keeps while the sheet scrolls (`keepsPlace`) and the cursor's move to a button that is shown when a smaller shape hides its own (`rescueButton`) are pure functions of `js/ui/academy-sandbox.js`. A control of the drawer that has scrolled out of its body gets no frame (the frame's shadow would dim the window with its hole off screen): the card says where it is and Show me scrolls to it. A lesson that ends on a control names it (`finish.anchor`), a step may stop the clock when it is done (`pause`: Other People's Money, so the Bank shows the glut's stress), and a first Deal Room stage with two Quick buttons carries the lesson's frame on the whole deal's. A lesson's HUD shows no level, XP or skill point of its own (`game.sandbox` marks a lesson's game), and `hud-clock` is the play button alone.

- **The news line.** With a side drawer open in a window that is both narrow and short (720px wide and 520px high at most, which is a 640x360 desktop window at 200% zoom) the ticker steps aside (`display: none`, `css/shell.css`), as it already did in the bottom sheet of a phone held upright: the strip between the camera column and the drawer is 103px there, and the headline got 37px of it ("Li...", "Day 2 T..."). It is back when the drawer shuts, an alert still opens over everything, the same words are read out, and the Academy's note for a step about the ticker says an open panel can cover it.

### Measuring the load
`node scripts/perf-load.mjs '{"base":"http://localhost:8791/Paperclip-Capitalist","cpu":4,"idle":6000}'` (serve the staged site first: `node scripts/stage.mjs _site --serve --port 8791`) takes the title, the draft and Begin as a player does, then prints when the world object, the first frame and the fully baked ground came, the long tasks, every change of the governor's tier, density and `lite`, and whether a buyer was ever missing from a frame; `profile` adds a CPU profile of the first two seconds. `cpu` slows the page's CPU that many times. The frame budget (`scripts/world-budget.mjs`) is judged on a software raster, which is a slow machine for the canvas and a fast one for JavaScript, and a Mac with the GPU on shows nothing: the governor's flapping and the seconds a slow laptop spent loading were invisible until the CPU was slowed 4x (an ordinary student laptop) and 6x.

What it found, on the staged site at 2x with Begin pressed 6 seconds into the draft, before this change and then after. At 1x: the first frame at 0.71 s then 0.33 s, the ground complete at 1.38 s then 0.95 s. At 4x: the first frame at 2.12 s then 0.75 s, the ground complete at 3.95 s then 2.54 s, 6.6 s then 2.4 s of the main thread blocked, 13 governor changes in 14 seconds then 1. At 6x: 3.19 s then 1.16 s, 5.83 s then 3.84 s, 15 changes then 1. What is left is the ground itself: the first view bakes about fifty chunks, 0.9 s of CPU on a Mac, in the per-pixel base of `js/world/rasterbase.js` (`landShade` and `seaPass` are most of it) and the canvas copies of `ChunkCache.bake`. It is spread over frames and budgeted already; making it cheaper changes pixels the art was judged on, or moves it to a worker, and neither is a small change.

`node scripts/perf-transitions.mjs '{"base":"http://localhost:8791/Paperclip-Capitalist","cpu":4}'` is the same measurement for the three things that change the picture while a player watches: zooming (four mouse notches in, four out), time passing (the clock at 8x for 14 seconds) and a district opening. For each it prints the frame gaps, how many frames filled a cache and how much of that was the ground's bake, the governor's tiers and how many times a quarter of the picture changed in one frame. Before the governor stopped judging cache fills, a zoom took a Mac to tier 4 and a zoom out to tier 6, the lights, smoke and people went off and the canvas lost density, and the picture took ten seconds to come back; a district opening was a 450 ms frame. Now, on a Mac, all three stay at tier 0 and the district opening is a 100 ms frame. At 4x the tier no longer flaps (0 to 2) but a zoom is still frames of 50 to 100 ms and a district opening still has a 370 ms frame: the ground's chunks are baked again at every scale a zoom rests at, and all of the visible ones when a district opens (the terrain's colors depend on which districts are under cloud), 20 to 70 ms each. That is the cost the next change has to cut.

## The coach card covers nothing
The Academy's card is judged by a matrix, not by cases: `node scripts/academy-sweep.mjs` puts the lesson in every step of every lesson (the card after the last step included), at six window sizes (1280x800, 1024x768, 768x1024, 844x390, 640x360, 360x640), with a mouse and with a finger (a coarse pointer: every button is 44px), in every state the player can put the game in (no panel; each panel the lesson can open, at the top and at the foot of its scroll; each stage of each Deal Room the lesson's setup allows; the hire dialog with and without a manager picked; the Cash breakdown; the lesson menu; an alert) and in six variants (the card as it arrives, with the 20-second hint, with toasts over the game, at the largest text size, asking "Skip this lesson?", and closed early). It measures each cell in the page and prints a table of what is wrong, by problem and by size, and exits 1 if anything is: a control (any button, link, field, summary, row with a `data-act`; the HUD, the tracker, the news line, the camera and list buttons, the dock, a panel's title bar, Close and tabs, its body, a window's own) that the card covers by a pixel or a sampled tap on which lands on it (`covers-<kind>`), the thing the step names covered, the words or the hint not on the screen at a readable size and in the window (`words-hidden`, `hint-hidden`), one of the card's own buttons missing, cut or under something (`own-*`), a tab with no name that says what it opens (`tab-unnamed`), a card outside the window, a toast on the words. `--level quick|std|full` says how much of the matrix to run (quick: the card as it arrives and with its hint in the main states; full: every variant in every state), `--lessons`, `--sizes`, `--modes`, `--steps`, `--states` and `--variants` narrow it, `--json` keeps the faults. The page half (`scripts/academy-sweep-page.js`) reads the page and decides nothing; the judge (`judge`) is pure and tested (`tests/academy-sweep.test.mjs`); the cut-down matrix (two sizes, four lessons) and real taps at the places the audit found by hand are `tests/academy-matrix.test.mjs`. The page is given `pc.acProbe` (set before a lesson starts: `js/ui/academy.js` hands it `ctx`, a jump to a step, the card's flags, the target and the card), so a lesson never has to be played to be measured; nothing in the game sets it. Animations and transitions are stopped in the swept page, so a card is measured where it stands, not where it is gliding to. Chrome's tabs that are not in front do not run animation frames, so the sweep runs one page at a time. A card the player asked for (opened from its tab, or asking "Skip this lesson?") is theirs: it must show everything and every button must work, and it may cover what it covers.

What the sweep found on the code before this rule (quick level, 9,426 cells at the same sizes): 4,590 cells with a fault, 7,730 faults of the card over a camera or list button, 1,940 hints and 1,626 sets of words drawn at 0 by 0 or cut by the card's own scroll, 683 missing buttons, 672 over the goal tracker, 392 over a panel's buttons, 380 over a panel's title bar and Close button, 133 over the thing the step names and 66 over the HUD. The rule is what makes those impossible rather than rare:
- **Every control is a control.** `keepRects` asks the page for every control of what the player can reach (`CONTROL_SELECTOR` in `js/ui/academy-sandbox.js`: the game screen, or only the open window's own when one is open, never what is behind it), each cut to the part of it that can be seen (a panel's body scrolls: a control scrolled out of it counts for nothing), and the control the keyboard is on above all. All are hard (`ctl`): a card is shown only where it touches none of them, the control the step names or the cursor. The open panel, a window's title bar and what a lesson asks the player to read count for less: the card prefers the empty part of the stage and stands over them only where nothing is emptier. A hand-written list of the parts the card keeps off (the old way: a panel's body, the dock, the HUD, with the camera, the tracker and a panel's own title bar left to their weights) is how every control that was not on it was covered. The sweep holds its own, broader, list of controls (`tests/academy-sweep.test.mjs`: the placer's selector is a superset of it).
- **Where it stands.** `placeCard` tries below, above and beside the target (a caret reaches 11px past the edge it faces, `CARET`, and a card that was pushed against the window's edge until its caret was over the target is not beside it), then a float above the dock, and then `freeSpots`: an exact search of every place a card of that size can stand without touching a blocker (a clear place slides to rest against a blocker or an edge, so those are the candidates), nearest to where the card would like to be, then the one clear of the controls that covers the least of the rest. Only a window that is controls from edge to edge has no such place, and then the card is not shown (below).
- **Sizes.** Full; slim (the words, the hint once it is due, the buttons, without the notes under the words); narrow (slim in a 15rem column). Every size shows the step's words, the hint and every button: there is no shape without the words (the head-and-buttons shapes `compact` and `min` that drew them at 0 by 0 are gone). `.ac-coach-in` is as tall as its words, up to the window (it was capped at a third to a half of it and scrolled the hint away), and a size whose words would still scroll inside it is not clear (`cut`, measured by the runner). `chooseSize` takes the first size that is clear of the cursor, the target, every control and `cut`; where none is, the tab.
- **The tab.** Where no size is clear (a phone's open panel, a Deal Room of buttons, the plain screen of a small window with the hint showing) the card folds to its tab (`handle`; the key C reaches it): "Step 2 of 4" or, once the hint is due, "Hint", with the step's first sentence or the hint itself on one line where the strip gives it the width. A tap opens the full card, wherever it covers least (it is the player's own doing); where the full card's words and hint are taller than the window (the largest text on a phone on its side: 544px of a 369px card at 844x390) it opens as the same words across the strip's width (`wide`, then `wide` without the notes: `OPEN_SIZES`, the first that does not scroll), so the hint is never behind the card's own scroll (`tests/academy-matrix.test.mjs` M3); and Fold away, a press elsewhere or the next step puts it back; a card that would stand clear after a fold waits two beats first (`unfold`), so a sheet that is scrolling does not make it flicker. The words and the hint stay in the page, clipped and never `display: none`; the fold is announced once a step in each window ("Press C, then Enter, to open it"); the tab's name says what it opens. Every card may fold, a closed lesson's included; only the question "Skip this lesson?" never does (the player has just pressed Skip lesson, and the question stays where the answer is). A card the player puts away (Fold away, or a press anywhere else) stays a tab for that step in that window or panel (`held`): it does not come back by itself because the cursor moved off a control, which is what un-blocks a place for the full card (the walk in `tests/rep-card.test.mjs`, "the folded card").
- **The strip.** A tab has to stand where no control does, and in a small window (`smallWindow`: under 44rem high or 46rem wide in the page's own rem, so 844x390, 640x360 and 360x640 at the default text size, and a laptop at the largest) nothing is guaranteed to be free. So for as long as a lesson is on screen `html.ac-strip` makes the bottom gutter (`--edge-b`) deeper by `--ac-strip` (a button's height and .75rem): the dock, the drawer, the news line and the toasts all stand above it, every window ends above it (`dialog#dlg { inset-block-end }`, and the Deal Room's heights), the camera and list buttons end above it, and the tab is placed in it (`tabSpot`), so it cannot be under anything. The shell measures its pieces by their sizes, and the strip moves the dock without resizing it, so the card asks it to measure again (`ctx.relayout`). A large window has no strip: its layout is the one it always was (the 1280x800 pictures of the plain screen and of the Deal Room are the same before and after, and the card is placed in two of six measured states where it was over a control before), and a clear place for the tab is found there by the same search (the sweep holds it for every cell).
- **What the strip must not break.** A window that is 50px shorter must still show what a player has to press. The Deal Room's result keeps its Continue row at the foot of the sheet with `position: sticky`, but a sticky row is held inside the block it is in, so while that block's top was still below the fold the row stood half cut off by the sheet's edge (a lesson's result at 640x360): after every paint `revealFoot` (`js/ui/deal.js`) scrolls the sheet just far enough that the whole row is in (lessons 1, 2, 4 and 7 in `tests/rep-card.test.mjs` fail without it). The dock's panels are `overflow: clip`, and their body is the containing block of what is placed in it: a screen-reader-only span at the end of the Market's list, placed against the panel itself, had made the panel 52px scrollable, so a script's `scrollIntoView` or the browser's own scroll for the focus of a press slid the title bar and the Close button out of sight and moved the list from under the finger between the press and the release (lesson 8 at 844x390; `tests/drawer-clip.test.mjs`, which also fails when the clip is taken out of the page).

## Sticky bars and the keyboard
A bar that sticks to an edge of what scrolls (the Crew hire dialog's foot, the Stock selection bar, the Travel ship row, the Quests jump bar, the world list's heading, the draft's actions bar) would stand over the control Tab scrolls to that edge: Tab scrolls only as far as the scroller's `scroll-padding` says. Each such bar carries `data-stuck="top"` or `"bottom"`, and `fitStuck(box)` in `js/ui/common.js` writes how far the bars reach into the box as `--stuck-t` and `--stuck-b`; the sheets turn them into the box's scroll-padding (`css/styles.css` for the page and every dialog, `css/shell.css` for the drawers and the world list). A bar wraps, so its height is read from the page, never written as a number in a sheet: `dlg.open` does it when a dialog opens and on resize (`dlg.fit()` after a repaint), the shell after a drawer is drawn and when anything resizes, the world list when it is built, the draft when its bar changes. A bar that leaves no room beside it gets `data-free` and scrolls with the content (`[data-stuck][data-free]` is static), because no padding makes room for a control next to it: one that would take more than two fifths of its scroller (the Ship row is 150px of a 200px drawer on a phone on its side), or one that leaves less than the tallest control needs (a 253px manager card beside the hire dialog's 83px foot in a 340px dialog). The Deal Room keeps its own fixed `scroll-padding` and `keepInView`. `tests/rep-feet.test.mjs` accounts for every `position: sticky` in the sheets (a new one that is neither measured, the Deal Room's nor a column fails) and walks every one of these surfaces with the real Tab and Shift+Tab at 1024x768, 844x390 (3x), 360x640 (3x) and 640x360 (2x).

## The staged site
GitHub Pages serves files, so what is published is built, checked and then uploaded, and it is not a copy of the repository. The pipeline is two scripts, one workflow and one test file.

```
node scripts/stage.mjs [out] [--lastmod YYYY-MM-DD] [--verify] [--serve [--port N] [--latency MS]]
node scripts/preload.mjs [--json]
```

**What is staged.** An allowlist: `index.html` (the source page with a block of `<link rel="modulepreload">` lines added to its head), `404.html`, `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt`, `assets/` (without `assets/src/`, the scenes the share image and the touch icon are drawn from: `node assets/src/render.mjs` redraws them in headless Chrome), `css/` (only the sheets `index.html` links) and `js/` (only the modules `js/main.js` imports). Never staged: `tests/`, `server/`, `docs/`, `scripts/`, `dev/`, the package files, `.git`, `.github`, any dotfile, any source map. A new top-level folder is therefore not published by accident, and a file in `js/` that nothing imports is not published either (today that is `js/meta/ids.js`, which only the tests use; `tests/finish-site.test.mjs` names it, so a second one has to be explained).

**The import graph and the preload lines.** A browser can only discover a module's imports after it has fetched and parsed that module, so a tree of ES modules loads one depth at a time. `scripts/preload.mjs` reads the source of every module the way JavaScript reads it (comments, strings, templates and regular expressions are skipped) and follows `import ... from`, `import 'x'`, `export ... from` and `import()`. A dynamic import whose argument is a template with a hole, ``import(`./play/${name}.js`)`` in `js/deal.js`, is read as a pattern: every file in `js/play/` that fits it is part of the graph, because any of them may be asked for. An import that resolves to no file, a bare specifier, or a dynamic import the scanner cannot read is an error, so a staged site never misses a module. The `<link rel="modulepreload">` lines are the static graph alone, in breadth-first order: all of it is needed before `js/main.js` can run. The modules that only `import()` reaches are the later screens': `js/main.js` loads the game (`js/ui/shell.js` and the world), the Academy, the instructor's dashboard and the debrief the first time each opens, so they are not preloaded (the title screen would wait for them) but listed apart, in `<script type="application/json" id="pc-lazy">`, which `prefetchRest` in `js/main.js` reads once the page has loaded and the browser is idle: eight modules at a time as low-priority module preloads, and nothing at all on a connection that asks for less data. A player who reads the title and drafts two Backgrounds has the game in the module map by Begin; one who presses a button at once waits for that screen's modules alone, under a "Loading" note a screen reader reads. `js/deal.js` imports its two games statically (it used to `await import()` them at the top, one more depth for the page to wait through). The Academy's lessons, `js/meta/academy.js`, are a static import of `js/ui/academy.js` and of `js/ui/drawers/codex.js`, so a deploy that misses the file fails at once and does not leave an empty Academy under a title screen that advertises ten missions. The source `index.html` has no preload lines, so `npm start` and the tests see the plain page; `stage.mjs` adds them to the staged copy only, after the stylesheets, which block the first paint and are asked for first. When the last batch has been asked for, `warmWorld` imports `js/world/scene.js` in idle time, so the island's layout, its coast and the opening ground's colors (about half a second of the world's first frame on a Mac, four times that on a slow laptop) are built while the title and the draft are on screen; `pc:coast` and `pc:scene` are their User Timing marks, and B4 in `tests/page-budget.test.mjs` waits for them with no world made yet. A player who presses Begin before the warm-up has run waits for the same work as before.

**The sitemap's date.** `--lastmod` sets every `<lastmod>`; the workflow passes the date of the commit being deployed (`git log -1 --format=%cs`), so the sitemap says when the site last changed without anyone editing it.

**The check.** `stage.mjs` runs `verify` on what it wrote and exits 1 on a problem: every top-level entry is one the site has, no dotfile or source map, no page asks another host for anything, every local file a page links is there, the module graph of the staged `js/` is the `js/` folder exactly (nothing missing, nothing left over), the preload lines are the static graph and the `pc-lazy` list is the rest, the sheets staged are the sheets linked, no text file writes the address in a spelling GitHub Pages does not answer to (the repository is `Paperclip-Capitalist` and a Pages address is case-sensitive: the same address in lower case is a 404 on the real host and nowhere else, so `addressProblems` reads every staged text file for it and for any `github.io` address that is not under `SITE_URL`), and `assets/share.png` is a 1200 x 630 PNG. `--verify DIR` runs the check alone on an existing folder.

**The workflow** (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) has two jobs. `test` installs the relay's dependencies, runs `npm test` and stops if a test is marked `todo`. `deploy` needs it, runs `node scripts/stage.mjs _site --lastmod "$(git log -1 --format=%cs)"` and uploads `_site`. The Actions versions are the ones the workflow was written with.

**Looking at it before it is live.** `npm run preview` stages and serves the folder the way a GitHub project site answers: only under `/Paperclip-Capitalist/`, spelled as GitHub spells the repository and matched case for case, below the mount too (every step of a path is looked up in its folder's own listing, `existsExactly` in `scripts/preload.mjs`, because the disk of a Mac does not tell `JS/` from `js/` and the disk Pages serves from does; the import walk reads the same way), so a path that only works at the root of a server fails here, and so does a lower-case leftover of the address or a file path in another case, `/Paperclip-Capitalist` redirecting to the slash, the site's own `404.html` with status 404 for any address that is not a file, at any depth, gzip for text, `cache-control: max-age=600`. `--latency MS` delays every answer, to feel a slow host.

**What was measured.** The sizes are those of the finished tree, staged by `node scripts/stage.mjs` (1 KB is 1,024 bytes; the page and every sheet and module gzipped on its own, level 6), and `tests/finish-repair.test.mjs` holds them to the staged site within 1%, so they cannot go stale the way the launch pass's figures did (1,423 KB of JavaScript and 150 KB of CSS at commit 2dfebc2). The times are from the launch pass (commit 2dfebc2; 2026-10-28, headless Chrome, cache off, the median of seven loads of a fresh profile, served by the stand-in on this machine, time to the title screen's heading): The module count has grown since the launch pass, from 57 to 83, because the world is now one module per layer or family (docs/RENDER_PLAN.md); the timing rows below were measured on the launch pass's tree and have not been measured again.
