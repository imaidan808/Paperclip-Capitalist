## Engine contract
`new Game({perks: [a, b], specialty, handle, pid, startHour, seed})`, where `perks` are the ids of exactly two of the twelve Backgrounds in `js/data/perks.js` (an unknown id is dropped, so the game would start without it), then `game.advanceTo(hour)` and the action methods (`pitch`, `buyLot`, `repair`, `sellPool`, `ship`, `hirePipeline`, `draw`, `repay`, `resolveAudit`, `exit`, `createOffer`/`acceptOffer`/`finalizeOffer`). Every action returns `{ok, msg, ...}`. State is a plain JSON object (`game.s`); the RNG seed is part of it, so a save replays identically. The environment (`game.env`) injects the cohort, difficulty and world seed. Quests, achievements, daily bounties and the Codex live in `game.s.meta` and are scored by `Progress` (`js/meta/progress.js`) from the events the engine emits (`game.onEvent`).

## The ledger
`game.s.ledger` is a **gain-and-cost ledger, not a cash book**, on purpose: a row is what an action gained or cost against fair value, and some rows are not cash. A sale's row is the gain over the crate's fair value (received minus fair value), a pool sale is booked whole when it is made (the cash lands after the delay), a classmate swap is the difference in value between what came in and what went out, and a manager's principal in flight has no row until the cycle ends; every other cash movement has its own row (buy, repair, fees, loans, repayments, fines, rent). So cash minus the sum of the rows is not zero, and it is not meant to be: the Bank drawer says so under the ledger ("a line is a gain or a cost, not always a cash payment"), the full rules are in the comment above `Game.led`, and the Debrief reads the rows as evidence of what the student did. A reconciling cash statement is not built; if students need one it is a separate view over the same state (cash movements only), not a change to these rows.

## Room protocol (JSON over either transport)
| Message | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `hello`, `bye` | anyone | presence |
| `snap` | student | public snapshot: net worth, skills, 48h niche counts, shock log, public inventory. What it says is believed only within bounds: the score is recomputed, an online founder is placed at the class's hour (within two days), and net worth is cut to what the best honest run reaches on that day (`nwCeiling`, `js/cohort.js`: $100 on day 1, $1.2M on day 30, $40M on day 90), so a made-up fortune is no better than the best real one could be by then and no better; the server tally below is the real fix |
| `clock` | instructor | `{hour, speed, paused, difficulty, ghosts, algo}`; students adopt only the instructor's (or, with no instructor, the lowest Founder ID's) |
| `inject`, `rewind` | instructor | deploy a shock (idempotent by id) / restore daily checkpoints |
| `offer`, `accept`, `confirm`, `deliver`, `ack`, `decline`, `withdraw`, `recall`, `holding`, `free` | student | peer swap in three steps: the proposer escrows and makes the offer; the addressee asks to `accept`; the proposer `confirm`s that it still holds the escrow (and holds it for them: it cannot be withdrawn or expire under them, once per try and twice per offer); only then does the addressee commit (and not at all on a confirmation that comes after its own 30-second wait) and `deliver`. The addressee has committed when it delivers, so the proposer takes any reply that names the crates it asked for (by id and class) and the exact cash, whatever district a crate is in now, credited as it described each crate and no better; only a reply that crates and cash together are worth under 45% of the description is refused. It answers `ack`, and the addressee sends the delivery again (five quick tries over 80 seconds, then every 30 seconds for half an hour; a committed swap is kept in the save, so a reload does not lose it) until it hears one. The proposer's hold on its escrow ends with a question and not a guess: when the hold has run 155 seconds (`LOCK_MS`: the acceptor's five quick tries run to 5 s x 5 x 5 = 125 seconds, and a confirmation can take the acceptor's own 30-second wait to arrive) it sends `recall`, and an addressee that has committed answers `holding` (and sends the delivery again at once, which renews the hold for five minutes, six times at most) while one that has not answers `free` (and forgets the offer, so a late confirmation cannot commit); only a `free`, 60 seconds of silence (`RECALL_WAIT_MS`) or a seventh lapse lets the owner withdraw or the engine expire the offer. A delivery lost for any time up to the half hour is therefore taken, and nothing is made twice. A swap whose addressee was confirmed is finished even if the bell rang meanwhile |
| `flag` | student | a blocked offer outside +/-40% of fair value |
| `royalty` | student | daily payment on a royalty stream. The payer's day's rate falls due for each day of the stream and is paid from the cash there is, in at most twelve messages a day (`ROYALTY_MSGS`), each at most a day's rate; what cash cannot cover stays owed as arrears (a liability in its net worth and in an exit's proceeds) and is paid first from the next cash. A stream whose receiver's run is over, or who is selling the business, ends. The receiver takes at most a day's rate a message and never more than the stream's cap (the least of what was promised in all and the worth it was counted at plus the 40% band), and never faster than the days go by: by the class's clock, a day's rate for each full day the stream has run, plus the day in progress and one in hand for a clock that runs a little behind, so a payer cannot send the whole of it at once. A promised royalty counts for at most a tenth of its promiser's net worth (`ROYALTY_NW`), and one a day larger than the promiser's whole worth is declined |

## Who is who on the relay
A Founder ID is a name, not a proof: every message carries its sender's, so every classmate knows everyone's, the instructor's included. So a connection to the relay must bring `key`, a random secret its browser keeps beside the ID (`pc.key.<slot>` in localStorage). The first connection to use an ID in a room binds the ID to a hash of its key (nothing else of it is kept, and it is never logged); a later connection for that ID with another key, or none, is refused. That is what stops a classmate from taking the instructor's seat, or another student's name and snapshot, by connecting with an ID they have read off the wire. The instructor's seat is the first connection to claim it, kept for its ID (and so its key), for as long as the room lives: another ID is never handed it by waiting (a laptop lid closed for a break must not make a student the instructor), and the first holder takes it back from anyone. An operator who wants a handover after a long absence sets `SEAT_GRACE_MS` (milliseconds; 0, never, by default). The other way to the seat is the room's **owner token**: a random secret (32 hex characters) the instructor's browser makes when the room is opened and keeps beside the room's code. It is sent as `owner` in the connection, never to a student, and the relay keeps only a hash of it (`sha256` of the room code, `owner` and the token), as it keeps only a hash of a Founder ID's key. The first instructor to bring a token while the seat is free (or is its own) binds the room to it; from then on whoever brings the same token is the owner, from any device and under any Founder ID, and takes the seat from whoever sits in it. A token that does not match changes nothing, and a connection with none claims the seat as before. The **recovery code** the instructor is shown is this token in groups of four: it is what "Take back a room" asks for, with the room code. A recovered instructor does not know the class's hour, so it waits 15 seconds for a student's clock and otherwise starts paused at the hour the class's snapshots show (`RECOVER_WAIT_MS`). A connection that closes is announced to the room as a `bye`, and a pid that connected, never sent a snapshot and has been gone 30 seconds is forgotten when the table of pids is full, so a script that greets and leaves cannot fill it. A full relay forgets the room that has been empty longest before it turns a class away. Two tabs of one browser with the same slot share both. Over a browser's own tabs (`BroadcastChannel`) there is no relay and no key: every tab is the same browser, and it is not a place to defend against.

## What a classmate's offer is checked against
A swap offer arrives from a classmate's browser, so what it says is checked against what that classmate has published to the room, before it is shown to anyone (`Session._publishedCovers`, `_onOffer`):
- **The offer against the published stock.** Every crate an offer describes must be in the stock the sender last published in its snapshot, with the same id, class and condition. Together the crates cannot be worth more than `(max(0, published net worth) + published debt) x 1.5 + 5`, where the published net worth is first cut to the best a run could have on that day (`nwCeiling`) and the debt is counted because the crates are bought with it. An offer that fails is answered with `decline`, so an honest proposer's escrow comes back. This is a bound and not a proof: the stock is the sender's own word, so a made-up crate is something the whole room can see in the roster and is bounded by the worth the sender can claim, not something the receiver can prove false.
- **A royalty against the promiser's worth.** What a promised royalty is worth is the promiser's published net worth, never the one written in the offer, and a royalty a day larger than that whole worth (and than $1) is declined.
- **The fair-value band.** Before either side commits, `checkOffer` compares what goes out with what comes in at the fair value each side can see, and blocks a swap outside +/-40% (`FMV_BAND`).

## Moving to a scalable, authoritative backend
1. Run `engine.js` on the server per student (it has no DOM), accept actions over HTTP/WebSocket, and persist `game.s` per Founder ID in Postgres with point-in-time recovery.
2. Move the cohort aggregation (`cohort.js`) server-side so niche volumes cannot be spoofed.
3. Validate peer trades server-side with the same `checkOffer`. Without it a client is still believed about one thing: the cash and the crates it says it handed over (the proposer credits what the reply says, clamped to the offer, but cannot check the other side really gave them up), so a hostile classmate can still take what a proposer escrowed for a crate it never parted with. Two sides' reports of the same swap, cross-checked, are the cheapest fix.
   The same goes for the other direction, the crate a proposer says it is giving. The client keeps a bound, not a proof: an offer is shown only for crates the sender has listed in its own published stock (same id, class and condition) and worth no more than 1.5 times its published net worth (cut to the best run of that day) plus its debt, so a made-up crate is something the whole room can see in the roster, and bounded by the worth the sender claims. A client-side "two-sided" check was considered and not built (the repair round of the v2 audit): the proposer's escrow record would travel with the offer, but it is the proposer's own word again, so a colluding pair proves nothing to each other's engine. Only the server tally proves it.
4. Add an LTI 1.3 launch to mint the pseudonymous Founder ID and an Assignment and Grade Services call from the gradebook export.
5. Keep the front end unchanged: swap `net.js`'s relay for the API.

## The 2.5D world
`World` (js/world/world.js) is a Canvas 2D renderer with no dependencies. The island is a tile grid in isometric projection (`sx = (gx - gy) * 32`, `sy = (gx + gy) * 16 - gz`); the sixteen districts sit on plateaus at different elevations joined by roads, bridges and a ferry. Each game tick `sync()` reconciles *entities* with live game state: buyer NPCs per lead (they walk to a slot and walk away when the lead goes), pallets per lot, crates per stored asset, buildings per pipeline (height = level), empty plots, trucks per freight run. Static scenery is a deterministic list in `layout.js`. The frame loop draws ground, then depth-sorted props and entities, then screen-space passes (night multiply, lights, rain, labels, bubbles). Hit shapes are recorded while drawing and picked back-to-front.

Rules that keep it honest: the world never changes game state itself (clicks go through `api.activate` to the same `acts` as the dashboard); one broken object cannot blank the scene (each item draws inside try/catch and logs once); the clock-driven day/night is rate-limited and fixed at fast clock speeds (no strobing); reduced motion draws only on change; quality drops automatically (30 to 20 to 15 fps, lower device-pixel ratio) on a slow machine. `tests/world.test.mjs` draws every entity type, district, mode, hour and shock against a recording canvas.

### Presentation notes (the polish pass)
These are drawing and layout rules only; none of them reads or changes game state.
- **Pixel density.** The canvas is drawn at the screen's density to 2x. Where the 2D canvas is on the CPU (a browser with the GPU off or emulated: it refuses a WebGL context asked for with `failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat`, see `softwareRaster` in `gfx.js`) the cap is 1.5x, because every full-window layer is a pass over all of its pixels on the main thread. The quality governor takes a further quarter off at its fourth tier and gives it back below its second, so a picture on the edge never reallocates the canvas back and forth.
- **Light layers.** `Lighting` makes at most one full-size layer a frame (`perFrame`), so the first dusk spreads its night, crossfade and shafts over three frames instead of one 70 ms frame; a frame without its crossfade stamps the day's air alone. The shafts are baked into that crossfade (`Lighting.mix`, eight steps of their strength) and are never stamped on their own: on a CPU-drawn canvas a plain full-window stamp costs 0.9 to 1.6 ms and a stamp or fill in the additive or multiply composites 3.0 to 3.6, so a dusk frame is one plain stamp, the grade's one multiply tint and the light pools, and `World.draw()` at dusk is about 1 ms where it was 17 (the raster of the additive stamp ran inside it).
- **Sprites.** A zoom that is moving shows the prop's sprite of the zoom before, scaled; a sprite of more than 2.2 Mpx is built only once the scale has rested; the memory budget grows with the density. `World.detach()` gives every bitmap back (`release()`).
- **The camera buttons.** They stand over one edge of the window, so `fit()`, the camera clamp, the district name tags and the map's cards use the part they leave free (`World.inset`, measured by `worldview.js` from the real buttons).
- **Dialogs and the Academy card.** A closed `<dialog>` is never drawn (`dialog:not([open]) { display: none }`, and `dlg` takes its class off when it closes), and when the control that opened it is gone the cursor goes to the open drawer's title, else to the game. The Deal Room's entrance animation fills `backwards`, never `both`: a held `transform: none` makes the sheet the containing block of every fixed box inside it, the coach layer among them. The coach card follows into any open Deal Room (a step about a buyer or lot in the world is shown there with no ring), the layer is marked `data-keep` so `setHTML` leaves it where it is when the sheet repaints, and `placeCard` takes rects to stay off and ranks the places by harm: the control the keyboard is on first, then the control the step names (never covered while any place in the window is clear of it: a card that says "open Market" over Market cannot be obeyed with a mouse or a finger), then the fewest buttons (the drawer's and the dock's), then the least weighted area (`coverScore`: the drawer and dock, the game's buttons, and in a dialog its own controls). The card has four sizes (the whole card; a slim one, the step's words and the buttons without the notes; a compact one, its head, hint and buttons; a minimal one, its head and two buttons) and `chooseSize` takes the largest that stands clear of the control the keyboard is on, the control the step names and, over a drawer, the buttons; it is placed again whenever the drawer scrolls, and what a lesson asks the player to read in a Deal Room (the seller's reply, the result line, the footer's cells) counts for less than a control, so the card prefers the empty part of the stage. A control of the drawer that has scrolled out of its body gets no frame (the frame's shadow would dim the window with its hole off screen): the card says where it is and Show me scrolls to it. A lesson that ends on a control names it (`finish.anchor`), a step may stop the clock when it is done (`pause`: Other People's Money, so the Bank shows the glut's stress), and a first Deal Room stage with two Quick buttons carries the lesson's frame on the whole deal's. A lesson's HUD shows no level, XP or skill point of its own (`game.sandbox` marks a lesson's game), and `hud-clock` is the play button alone.

## The staged site
GitHub Pages serves files, so what is published is built, checked and then uploaded, and it is not a copy of the repository. The pipeline is two scripts, one workflow and one test file.

```
node scripts/stage.mjs [out] [--lastmod YYYY-MM-DD] [--verify] [--serve [--port N] [--latency MS]]
node scripts/preload.mjs [--json]
```

**What is staged.** An allowlist: `index.html` (the source page with a block of `<link rel="modulepreload">` lines added to its head), `404.html`, `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt`, `assets/` (without `assets/src/`, the scenes the share image and the touch icon are drawn from: `node assets/src/render.mjs` redraws them in headless Chrome), `css/` (only the sheets `index.html` links) and `js/` (only the modules `js/main.js` imports). Never staged: `tests/`, `server/`, `docs/`, `scripts/`, `dev/`, the package files, `.git`, `.github`, any dotfile, any source map. A new top-level folder is therefore not published by accident, and a file in `js/` that nothing imports is not published either (today that is `js/meta/ids.js`, which only the tests use; `tests/finish-site.test.mjs` names it, so a second one has to be explained).

**The import graph and the preload lines.** A browser can only discover a module's imports after it has fetched and parsed that module, so a tree of ES modules loads one depth at a time. `scripts/preload.mjs` reads the source of every module the way JavaScript reads it (comments, strings, templates and regular expressions are skipped) and follows `import ... from`, `import 'x'`, `export ... from` and `import()`. A dynamic import whose argument is a template with a hole, ``import(`./play/${name}.js`)`` in `js/deal.js`, is read as a pattern: every file in `js/play/` that fits it is part of the graph, because any of them may be asked for. An import that resolves to no file, a bare specifier, or a dynamic import the scanner cannot read is an error, so a staged site never misses a module. Static modules are listed in breadth-first order, then the ones only `import()` brings in (`js/deal.js` loads `haggle.js` and `auction.js` with a top-level `await`, so the page needs them before it can start). The Academy's lessons, `js/meta/academy.js`, are a static import of `js/ui/academy.js` and of `js/ui/drawers/codex.js`, so a deploy that misses the file fails at once and does not leave an empty Academy under a title screen that advertises ten missions. The source `index.html` has no preload lines, so `npm start` and the tests see the plain page; `stage.mjs` adds them to the staged copy only, after the stylesheets, which block the first paint and are asked for first.

**The sitemap's date.** `--lastmod` sets every `<lastmod>`; the workflow passes the date of the commit being deployed (`git log -1 --format=%cs`), so the sitemap says when the site last changed without anyone editing it.

**The check.** `stage.mjs` runs `verify` on what it wrote and exits 1 on a problem: every top-level entry is one the site has, no dotfile or source map, no page asks another host for anything, every local file a page links is there, the module graph of the staged `js/` is the `js/` folder exactly (nothing missing, nothing left over), the preload lines are that graph, the sheets staged are the sheets linked, and `assets/share.png` is a 1200 x 630 PNG. `--verify DIR` runs the check alone on an existing folder.

**The workflow** (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) has two jobs. `test` installs the relay's dependencies, runs `npm test` and stops if a test is marked `todo`. `deploy` needs it, runs `node scripts/stage.mjs _site --lastmod "$(git log -1 --format=%cs)"` and uploads `_site`. The Actions versions are the ones the workflow was written with.

**Looking at it before it is live.** `npm run preview` stages and serves the folder the way a GitHub project site answers: only under `/paperclip-capitalist/` (so a path that only works at the root of a server fails here), `/paperclip-capitalist` redirecting to the slash, the site's own `404.html` with status 404 for any address that is not a file, at any depth, gzip for text, `cache-control: max-age=600`. `--latency MS` delays every answer, to feel a slow host.

**What was measured.** The sizes are those of the finished tree, staged by `node scripts/stage.mjs` (1 KB is 1,024 bytes; the page and every sheet and module gzipped on its own, level 6), and `tests/finish-repair.test.mjs` holds them to the staged site within 1%, so they cannot go stale the way the launch pass's figures did (1,423 KB of JavaScript and 150 KB of CSS at commit 2dfebc2). The times are from the launch pass (commit 2dfebc2; 2026-10-28, headless Chrome, cache off, the median of seven loads of a fresh profile, served by the stand-in on this machine, time to the title screen's heading): The module count has grown since the launch pass, from 57 to 83, because the world is now one module per layer or family (docs/RENDER_PLAN.md); the timing rows below were measured on the launch pass's tree and have not been measured again.

**What is not done, and why.** The page still loads every module before the title screen, because `js/main.js` imports every screen statically and one module awaits an `import()` while it evaluates. The title screen alone needs 16 modules, 371 KB of JavaScript (127 KB gzipped); the other 79, 1,327 KB (467 KB gzipped), are the game world, the Academy, the Codex, the instructor dashboard and the debrief, which nobody needs until they press a button. Loading them with `import()` from `js/main.js` when a screen opens would defer three quarters of the bytes. It is not done because it is a change to the game's entry point, not to the site's pipeline, and it needs its own proof that every screen opens the same way; `scripts/preload.mjs` already understands `import()`, so the pipeline needs nothing for it, and it would move those modules from the static list to the dynamic one. Separately, the one `import()` that blocks module evaluation with a top-level `await` (`js/deal.js`'s optional games) is a guard from when those games arrived on a separate branch; made static it would join the first fetch instead of waiting for it, and the preload lines already take most of the cost out of it. (The Academy's lessons were two more such guards, in `js/ui/academy.js` and `js/ui/drawers/codex.js`, with comments saying they go at the merge; they are static imports now, held by `tests/finish-repair.test.mjs`.)

**Tests.** `tests/finish-site.test.mjs`: the scanner on every form of import and on every static import of the real app; the walk and its failures on a scratch project; the staged tree against the graph, file by file; `verify` against a staged tree with a module deleted, a stray file, a source map and a changed preload list; the Pages stand-in; the workflow (read by a small YAML reader written in the test and checked against PyYAML where it is installed); the head tags, JSON-LD, share image, 404 page, `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml` and `llms.txt`; the docs; and one boot of the staged site in headless Chrome (skipped without Chrome, or with `PC_NO_BROWSER=1`): the title screen, the Academy's first lesson, a game started through the draft, the 404 page, no console error and no failed request.
