# Rules as implemented, and where the spec needed a decision

Every number lives in `js/config.js` (economy), `js/data/` (districts, assets, buyers, Backgrounds) and `js/market.js` (formulas). The design contract is `docs/V2_DESIGN.md`; this file says what the engine actually does and what was decided on the way.

## Time
1 virtual hour = 7 real seconds at 1x. 24 hours = 1 day, 90 days = 2,160 steps = 4.2 real hours at 1x. A classroom picks the pace: the instructor dashboard has presets from "1 day = 30 s" to "90 days in 28 days". The engine is deterministic (seeded RNG, state in the save): a student who is away catches up by replaying the missed hours.

## Formulas
| Rule | As built |
|---|---|
| Asset Utility Score | `scarcity x local demand - depreciation`; price index `0.5 + U/100`. Demand is per district (`dem` is a sparse object; no entry means no market there). |
| Creativity Multiplier | `[0.40 + ln((V_total+2)/(V_niche+1))] x (1 - S)`, clamped to **[0.40, 3.14]**, `S = min(1, V_niche/V_total)`. Volumes are the last 48 virtual hours, per asset class and district. |
| Pitch price | `reference x fit term x 0.60 x ECON.pitchK x Cm x Growth x Background x Deal Room`, capped at the buyer's budget. Reference is the asset's fair value times its hidden condition, or the stated budget of an urgent lead. |
| Deal Room | The games return a price multiplier within **[0.6, 1.4]** and a quality multiplier within **[0.85, 1.15]** (repair cost within [0.9, 1.3]); the engine clamps them again. Quick (auto-play at average skill) sells at 1.0 of the pitch's fair price and buys at 1.0 to 1.18 of the asking price (1.04 on average), which is the economy the bot is balanced on. |
| EEI | `ending net worth / max(start, floor) x (1 + days remaining / 90)`. Everyone starts with one $0.05 paperclip and $0 cash, so the floor ($1,000, instructor-adjustable) is a constant divisor and the score is a pure ranking of net worth and time. |
| Peer variance | `abs(a - b) / min(a, b)`, blocked above 40% (7.69% for $4,200 vs $3,900). |
| Repair | cost `base x 0.42^(Ops-1)`, times the district's repair discount and the Background and district bonuses; restored quality `0.5 + 0.5 x min(1, (Ops-1)/4)`, times the Deal Room's precision result. |
| Broken lots | A broken lot is posted at `max(brokenLo, 1 - brokenSlope x (Ops - 1))` of its as-is value (30% of a working crate's), so with `brokenLo` 1 and `brokenSlope` -0.21 a wreck costs 1.00 times its as-is value at Ops 1, 1.42 at Ops 3, 1.63 at Ops 4, 2.05 at Ops 6 and 2.47 at Ops 8 (the Licensed Tradesperson pays 20% less). A crate restored at Ops 5 or more sells for about 3.3 times its as-is value, so a flip returns about +80% at Ops 4 and +35% at Ops 8 before repair, friction and fees. From district 13 on a wreck costs `brokenTop` more for each district past 12 on top of that (x1.10 in Corporate Valley, x1.20, x1.30, x1.40 in the Global Exchange): the sellers of wrecks worth tens or hundreds of thousands know what a repaired crate fetches, so a flip at Ops 6 returns about +48% in district 13 and +16% in 16, and at Ops 8 about +4% in 15 and -4% in 16 (before repair, friction and fees). |
| Managers | Capital and hire cost scale with the district's gate (`(gate/1000)^0.62`), margin is the same in every district (`PIPE_RULES.marginBase` 1.15, `marginSlope` 0, floor 0.5), so a balanced niche (Cm 1.68) pays a Broker 7.8% of the allocation before costs, a Logistics Agent 6.3% and a Student 6.0%, cycle length is `ECON.cycleBase / district speed`. Gross = `allocation x margin x ECON.margin x (Cm/1.68) x lane`, less fee, tax, manager royalty and overhead. |
| Loans | Rate per 30 virtual days: `(2.5% index + spread) x Fintech discount`, spread adjusted by Backgrounds and district bonuses. Draws capped at D/E 3.0; margin call at 4.5 seizes the highest-value managers at a 35% discount until D/E is 3.0. |

## Equal starts
The v1 profiles ($0.05, $5,000 and $50,000, in three different districts) made scores incomparable. In v2 everyone starts with a paperclip and no cash. The difference between players is only the two Backgrounds they draft and what they do with them.

## Backgrounds
Each has a real-world basis, a strength and a cost (`js/data/perks.js`, `docs/V2_DESIGN.md` section 3). The pairing test is `node tests/balance.mjs [seeds] [every]` (`npm run balance`, five seeds and a decision every 4 virtual hours by default, about 2 to 4 minutes): it plays all 66 pairs with the heuristic bot, rotating the specialty, and reports the day each pair reaches districts 5, 9 and 13. A run that ends with no cash, no stock and no managers is a wipe-out: it has its own column and is left out of the days, because it did not run slowly, it stopped. Five seeds is the least that tells a slow pair from a bad draw (with two, the median is the mean of two runs and one wipe-out reads as "200% slow"). `node tests/balance.mjs 5 4 --no-managers` plays the same bot with hiring forbidden, and `node tests/balance.mjs 10 4 --compare` plays both and prints, for every Background (its eleven pairs), the day-90 median net worth of hand play over the median with managers (`handOverManagers()`): the number the design wants under 1 for every family at a decision every 4 and 8 hours. `node tests/tune.mjs '{"perks":{"native":{"digitalMult":1.05}}}' native,trades 4` sweeps one Background's numbers over three seeds (the key `perks` holds mod overrides by id, next to the `ECON` knobs and `pipe`), which is how the finishing round tried its candidates before it chose.

### What the audit round changed in the economy
Between commit 816e7c4 (before the audit) and commit 52340f0 the audit fixes, the retune after them and the wreck premium changed these numbers and rules; this round then touched three Backgrounds (the next two subsections). The measurements are the table above; the knobs are all in `js/config.js` and listed under Economy knobs.

| Rule | 816e7c4 | 52340f0 |
|---|---|---|
| Price of a broken lot, as a share of its as-is value | `max(0.8, 1 - 0.03 (Ops - 1))`: a wreck cost 1.00 times what it was worth as it lay at Ops 1 and 0.8 at Ops 8 (0d66995: `max(0.9, 1 - 0.015 (Ops - 1))`) | `max(1, 1 + 0.21 (Ops - 1))`, and 0.10 more for each district past 12: 1.00 at Ops 1, 1.63 at Ops 4, 2.47 at Ops 8, up to 3.46 in district 16 (`brokenLo`, `brokenSlope`, `brokenTop`, `brokenFrom`) |
| An urgent buyer's budget | fair value x (1.4 to 3.6) in every district | fair value x (1 + (0.4 to 2.6) x 3 x (1 - how far past district 4, over eight districts)): rich at the Scrap Yard, a trade price from district 12 (`urgentK` 3, `urgentFade` 1; 0d66995: 0.9 and 0.9) |
| Lots and buyers | a new lot every 1.5 x the district's interval, a buyer every 1.0 x (0d66995: 1.15) | every 1.1 x and every 0.8 x (`lotEvery`, `leadEvery`) |
| Buyers' price | the base pitch price (`pitchK` 1) | 8% under it (`pitchK` 0.92) |
| A manager's cycle and margin | `cycleBase` 80; margin `0.4 x (1 - 0.045 (district - 5))` | `cycleBase` 132; one margin for every district (`marginBase` 1.15, `marginSlope` 0, `margin` 1), so every district's best lane nets 5% to 9% a cycle before costs |
| Business-School Grad | no budget bonus | an alumni network: buyers bring 12% bigger budgets (`leadBudget` 1.12) |
| Skill points past mastery | a point nothing could take stayed banked as dead weight | worth `pointXp` 120 experience |
| The Second-Chance street sense | caught every counterfeit | misses `warnMiss` 0.1 of them |

Rules of the engine that moved money without being a knob (commit messages of 350eae2, 3e1bc42, aa0901d and 24f7fc6 have the test of each): the Enterprise commitment fee is charged once a month per facility, not once per draw; garnishment is taken only from money in hand and the rest is carried; there is no fraudulent buyer before the third trade; quotes are blind by default, so a player cannot read a crate's hidden condition for free; the Pawn District is 22% counterfeit and Auto Row 60% broken as the districts say, with the rolls shared out over the goods that can take them; a manager no longer crowds its own lane; a pioneer or herd verdict is never given to a sale that lost money; client contracts pay exactly their days; a skill game pays experience once per key and at most 300 a kind a day; a swap pays experience by the value that moved and counts at most three a day; crates in freight or escrow age at midnight; the run closes at the bell and at an exit; yields were raised so that every asset that pays out-earns its wear (Decisions the spec left open, 9).

What moved, in this round's own measurements. The first audit build (0d66995) was slower to start and ran away at the top: at a decision every 8 hours it reached district 5 on day 25 (the median of the pairs' days) and 19% of its runs came after day 30 (816e7c4: day 20.5 and 6%), and its day-90 median was 32%, 47% and 29% above 816e7c4's at every 2, 4 and 8 hours while the level was one lower; 25% of its runs at every 2 hours reached district 16 (816e7c4: 9%). This round's build reaches district 5 at every 8 hours on day 18 with 1% of the runs after day 30, and its day-90 median is 3% under, 6% over and 11% over 816e7c4's with the level back to 21, 19 and 16; district 13 comes on day 53 at the default rhythm (0d66995: 50.5) and district 16 is reached by 1% of the runs at every 2 hours.
The levers that did this are in Economy knobs. The reason is one fact: from the first weeks to the last, the Quick bot made its money by buying broken lots, repairing them and reselling (a restored crate is worth about 3.3 times the wreck), and a wreck cost less than its as-is value, so the flip returned about +200% a cycle at every stake. That is why the day-90 net worth at 0d66995 was 1.5 times 816e7c4's with a slower start, why the fast end ran away (a flip every few hours compounds), and why the same bot with managers forbidden out-earned the one with them. Wrecks now cost what sellers think they can become (1.00 times the as-is value at Ops 1 up to 2.05 at Ops 6), and the weeks they used to carry are paid back with richer urgent buyers and quicker lot and buyer flow.

### The Digital Native pocket
What the last verification found. The bot that never hires tied its own managers with the Digital Native pairs at a decision every 2 and 4 hours and beat them at every 8: on the 66-pair table of 52340f0 (five seeds) the day-90 median of hand play over the median with managers was 1.04x, 1.01x and 1.17x for the eleven pairs with Digital Native against 0.70x, 0.65x and 0.77x for the other 55 (on Native's eleven pairs alone with ten seeds, 0.96x, 0.99x and 1.13x). Hand play was ahead at the median of its five seeds in 8, 8 and 10 of the 66 pairs at 2, 4 and 8 hours, and 8, 7 and 7 of them were Native pairs.
Why. Every strength of the Digital Native is a hand-play strength, and the heaviest, digital goods +20% (`digitalMult`), is paid twice by the market: a lot is posted at a price that does not carry it (a sound lot at 97% of the plain fair value at Ops 1) and the crate is then valued, in the net worth and in every pitch, at 1.2 times that fair value, so a digital lot is worth about 24% over its price the moment it is bought. A manager earns its margin from `PIPE_RULES.marginBase` and never sees the premium; digital buyers in any district and lots that refresh 25% faster are hand play as well. Nothing in the audit round touched it: the wreck premium closed the same gap for every other pair and left this one open.

Chosen: `digitalMult` 1.10 and `scamRate` 0.12 (strength "digital goods +10% value", cost "12% of buyers are fraudulent unless you verify them"), with the 25% lot refresh and the 1.5% subscription as they were. It is the smallest change that puts 8 hours under 1 with room: each block of ten of the 30 seeds gives 0.89 at 8 hours and 0.71, 0.75 and 0.77 at 4. The last row (a third change, a slower lot refresh) would buy 0.04 more at 8 hours, which the table does not need.

## Hidden truth
A lot is posted "as described" at 97% of its fair value at Ops 1 (`lotDiscHi`), 2.5 points less for each Ops level above 1, never under 84% (`lotDiscLo`); a broken lot is posted far below a working one, at 1.00 to 2.47 times its own as-is value, and up to 3.46 times in district 16 (Formulas, Broken lots). Its real condition (a multiplier of 0.7-1.15, 0.85-1.15 in the first two districts) and whether it is counterfeit (the district's `fakes` rule is the share of its lots that are fake, shared out by each good's `fakeRisk` against the district's mean, so a risky good carries more of it; a fake is worth 8%) are hidden. A lot that is broken is never also fake, and an asset with no `fakeRisk` counts as 1. Net worth counts described value until the truth is known (a buyer's refusal, a pool sale, or reading it off an inspection). Buyers always check what they buy, so a fake is never sold at full price: information is worth money, which is what the Inspect game teaches.
What the rules add up to, measured on thousands of generated lots (`tests/audit-content.test.mjs` holds the same numbers): in the Pawn District 22% of the lots are fakes (about one in five), a watch (full `fakeRisk`) is fake 32% of the time at the gate (the district's rate is shared out over the kinds of goods on its counter, and that list widens with net worth: measured with $10M it is 36%), and 35% of the stock goes to auction; Estate Sales puts about 55% of its lots to auction; six in ten of Auto Row's lots arrive broken (about three in four of the ones that can be repaired); a quarter of Real Estate Row's lots are foreclosure auctions. A Sealed Mystery Box is worth 85% to 115% of what it looks like and is never fake, like any lot in the first two districts.
Two doors would let the truth out for nothing, and are shut. A swap refused on a crate's hidden worth says only that the crate cannot be traded at that price, and it costs 3% of the value it was refused at (`SWAPS.appraisal`, a ledger row of type `verify` and a count in `s.meta.appraisals`): asking for nothing, crate after crate, would be a free counterfeit detector that makes Read pointless. And the first move at a Close or a Bid in the Deal Room is final (`s.tried`): sat at before, the talk is played at Quick, so a negotiation cannot be left, read and played again with its answers in hand.

## Economy knobs (`ECON`)
Values in `js/config.js` today, each with what it does (the same table is the comment above `ECON`):
- `lotDiscHi` 0.97 and `lotDiscLo` 0.84: a sound lot is posted at 97% of its fair value at Ops 1, 2.5 points less for each Ops level above 1, never under 84%.
- `brokenLo` 1 and `brokenSlope` -0.21: a broken lot is posted at `max(brokenLo, 1 - brokenSlope x (Ops - 1))` of its as-is value. The slope is negative on purpose: sellers of wrecks ask for the repair upside, so a better repairer pays more (Formulas, Broken lots). It was 0.9 and 0.015, a wreck at 0.985 down to 0.9 of its as-is value (Ops 1 to 8), which made repairing and reselling worth +200% a cycle at every stake.
- `brokenTop` 0.1 and `brokenFrom` 12: from the district after `brokenFrom` a wreck is posted `brokenTop` dearer for each district further out, on top of the price above (x1.10 in district 13 up to x1.40 in 16; nothing below 13, so the early and middle game are exactly as they were). It is the lever on the one thing the other knobs could not reach: a bot that decides every two hours and never hires flipped a wreck after another in districts 15 and 16, where one is worth $100,000 and a restored crate fetches +60% to +100% over its price, and compounded that into a net worth 4.7 times the same bot's with managers (BALANCE.md, Managers and hand play). - `lotSlots` 4 and `lotEvery` 1.1: lots open at once in a district, and how much slower than the district's own `lotEvery` hours they come (it was 1.5).
- `leadEvery` 0.8: the same for buyers, 0.8 times the district's own interval (it was 1.15), so buyers come 44% more often.
- `urgentK` 3 and `urgentFade` 1: an urgent buyer's budget is fair value x (1 + (0.4 to 2.6) x `urgentK` x (1 - `urgentFade` x how far past district 4, over eight districts)): need pays well at the Scrap Yard, less each district further, and nothing extra from district 12 (it was 0.9 and 0.9, with a tenth of the premium left there). ## Decisions the spec left open (please review)
1. **Balanced Creativity value.** The spec's formula gives 2.36 for `V_niche=10, V_total=100` while its edge-case table says 1.68. The formula and its floor and ceiling (0.40, 3.14) are implemented; the buyer baseline is 0.60 of fair value so a "balanced" niche is worth about 1.0x, and managers normalize by 1.68.
2. **Interest rates** of 2.5% to 8.5% are per 30 virtual days, since a 90-day game would barely register an annual rate.
3. **Urgent leads.** A $0.05 paperclip cannot reach a $3 pen through cost-based pricing, so some leads are urgent: the buyer's stated budget is the reference value. The opening lead is guaranteed.
4. **Cohort volumes** come from real peers plus a deterministic ghost herd. The herd climbs the districts together as a moving bell (center at district `1 + 14 x (day/90)^0.9`) and drifts to a second-best niche every ten days.
5. **Exit.** M&A and IPO value the business at 6x or 10x trailing average daily profit plus book value (times Global Exchange's exit bonus), less 12% brokerage, settling after 48 hours. IPO needs a clean audit record.
6. **Algorithmic events** (viral mention, supply glut, regulatory scare) derive from the room code and day, so every student in a room sees the same ones.
7. **Skill points replace cash training.** Levels (`floor(sqrt(xp / LEVEL_XP)) + 1`, `LEVEL_XP` 150) grant one point each; skills top out at level 8, so a founder can spend 19 points at most (the Heir, with no specialty, 21). The points authored in content stay inside that: quests 8, the Academy 5 and six skill-game epics 1 each, 19 in all; the other epics pay experience only. The engine banks a point only if a skill can still take it (`Game.grantPoints`) and turns the rest into XP.
8. **Per-district rules the data authors added** beyond the required ones: small `fakes` rules in several mid and late districts so `fakeRisk` and Inspect matter there; `gray` markets in the Pawn District and the Global Exchange; `customs` at the Exchange so arbitrage has to beat freight.
9. **Assets that pay must out-earn their wear.** A held asset loses `dep` x 0.2% of its value a day (doubled for digital goods in the Tech Corridor, the fads district), so every asset with a `yield` pays at least 0.15 points a day more than that in the worst case (0.35% to 0.6% a day against wear of 0.2% to 0.4%), and holding any of them for 30 days raises net worth. Real Estate Row pays 25% more rent on top (`rentMult`). Its quest asks for $25,000 of income, which two apartment blocks bought on the day the district opens collect in about five days.
10. **Quick is par, and skill is rare.** On the sell side Quick closes at or below the pitch's fair price, never above; on the buy side it pays 1.0x to 1.18x the asking price, never under. So the rare haggle goals ask for what only skilled play reaches (a sale 10% or more above fair, a purchase 5% or more below the asking price), and the daily "Buy Low" asks for the asking price or lower, which Quick manages about half the time.
