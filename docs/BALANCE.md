# Rules as implemented, and where the spec needed a decision

Every number lives in `js/config.js` (economy), `js/data/` (districts, assets, buyers, Backgrounds) and `js/market.js` (formulas). The design contract is `docs/V2_DESIGN.md`; this file says what the engine actually does and what was decided on the way.

## Time
1 virtual hour = 7 real seconds at 1x. 24 hours = 1 day, 90 days = 2,160 steps = 4.2 real hours at 1x. A classroom picks the pace: the instructor dashboard has presets from "1 day = 30 s" to "90 days in 28 days". The engine is deterministic (seeded RNG, state in the save): a student who is away catches up by replaying the missed hours.

## Formulas
| Rule | As built |
|---|---|
| Asset Utility Score | `scarcity x local demand - depreciation`; price index `0.5 + U/100`. Demand is per district (`dem` is a sparse object; no entry means no market there). |
| Creativity Multiplier | `[0.40 + ln((V_total+2)/(V_niche+1))] x (1 - S)`, clamped to **[0.40, 3.14]**, `S = min(1, V_niche/V_total)`. Volumes are the last 48 virtual hours, per asset class and district. |
| Pitch price | `reference x fit term x 0.60 x ECON.pitchK x Cm x Growth x Background x Deal Room`, capped at the buyer's budget. Reference is the asset's fair value times its hidden condition, or the stated budget of an urgent lead. |
| Deal Room | The games return a price multiplier within **[0.6, 1.4]** and a quality multiplier within **[0.85, 1.15]** (repair cost within [0.9, 1.3]); the engine clamps them again. Quick (auto-play at average skill) sells at 1.0 of the pitch's fair price and buys at 1.0 to 1.18 of the asking price (1.04 on average), which is the economy the bot is balanced on. |
| EEI | `ending net worth / max(start, floor) x (1 + days remaining / 90)`. Everyone starts with one $0.05 paperclip and $0 cash, so the floor ($1,000, instructor-adjustable) is a constant divisor and the score is a pure ranking of net worth and time. |
| Peer variance | `abs(a - b) / min(a, b)`, blocked above 40% (7.69% for $4,200 vs $3,900). |
| Repair | cost `base x 0.42^(Ops-1)`, times the district's repair discount and the Background and district bonuses; restored quality `0.5 + 0.5 x min(1, (Ops-1)/4)`, times the Deal Room's precision result. |
| Broken lots | A broken lot is posted at `max(brokenLo, 1 - brokenSlope x (Ops - 1))` of its as-is value (30% of a working crate's), so with `brokenLo` 1 and `brokenSlope` -0.21 a wreck costs 1.00 times its as-is value at Ops 1, 1.42 at Ops 3, 1.63 at Ops 4, 2.05 at Ops 6 and 2.47 at Ops 8 (the Licensed Tradesperson pays 20% less). A crate restored at Ops 5 or more sells for about 3.3 times its as-is value, so a flip returns about +80% at Ops 4 and +35% at Ops 8 before repair, friction and fees. |
| Managers | Capital and hire cost scale with the district's gate (`(gate/1000)^0.62`), margin is the same in every district (`PIPE_RULES.marginBase` 1.15, `marginSlope` 0, floor 0.5), so a balanced niche (Cm 1.68) pays a Broker 7.8% of the allocation before costs, a Logistics Agent 6.3% and a Student 6.0%, cycle length is `ECON.cycleBase / district speed`. Gross = `allocation x margin x ECON.margin x (Cm/1.68) x lane`, less fee, tax, manager royalty and overhead. |
| Loans | Rate per 30 virtual days: `(2.5% index + spread) x Fintech discount`, spread adjusted by Backgrounds and district bonuses. Draws capped at D/E 3.0; margin call at 4.5 seizes the highest-value managers at a 35% discount until D/E is 3.0. |

## Equal starts
The v1 profiles ($0.05, $5,000 and $50,000, in three different districts) made scores incomparable. In v2 everyone starts with a paperclip and no cash. The difference between players is only the two Backgrounds they draft and what they do with them.

## Backgrounds
Each has a real-world basis, a strength and a cost (`js/data/perks.js`, `docs/V2_DESIGN.md` section 3). The pairing test is `node tests/balance.mjs [seeds] [every]` (`npm run balance`, five seeds and a decision every 4 virtual hours by default, about 2 to 4 minutes): it plays all 66 pairs with the heuristic bot, rotating the specialty, and reports the day each pair reaches districts 5, 9 and 13. A run that ends with no cash, no stock and no managers is a wipe-out: it has its own column and is left out of the days, because it did not run slowly, it stopped. Five seeds is the least that tells a slow pair from a bad draw (with two, the median is the mean of two runs and one wipe-out reads as "200% slow"). `node tests/balance.mjs 5 4 --no-managers` plays the same bot with hiring forbidden.

The managers are the passive route. The same bot with hiring forbidden (`--no-managers`), median day-90 net worth with managers against without, 330 runs a cell (pairs: every second pair of the table for the two older builds, all 66 for 31ddccf; wipe-outs left out):

|---|---|---|---|
| 2 hours | 1.51M against 39.93M (26.5x) | 1.99M against 16.87M (8.5x) | 1.38M against 6.28M (4.5x) |
| 4 hours | 1.01M against 27.36M (27x) | 1.50M against 8.09M (5.4x) | 1.03M against 870,000 (0.85x) |
| 8 hours | 435,000 against 1.04M (2.4x) | 525,000 against 418,000 (0.80x) | 444,000 against 377,000 (0.85x) |

So at the median of the 66 pairs, for a player who decides every 4 hours or less often, the managers now earn about as much as the hand play they displace (they lost 5 to 27 times before), and at a decision every 2 hours hand play still wins by 4.5 times, down from 8.5. That gap is open, and **"at least as much" is a statement about that median, not about every pair**: see the next paragraph. The fast end: the fastest rhythm turns a repaired crate over several times a day and the lots of districts 14 to 16 are worth hundreds of thousands, so a bot that decides every two hours finds district 16 in 70% of its runs without managers (22% at every 4, 1% at every 8) and ends with a net worth 13 times apart from its 10th to its 90th percentile. A liquidity pool that refills 10% a day instead of 35%, fewer lots (3 instead of 4) and a lot refresh twice as slow did not touch it; a refresh 3 to 5 times slower narrowed it only by taking a third to a half off the net worth of the bot with managers as well, and a dearer wreck (1.9 to 2.3 times the as-is value for everyone) closed it only at the cost of the early weeks and a spread of 4 times and more (tried on a 22-pair, 3-seed sample). A class cannot play at that rhythm: a day in 30 seconds is 1.25 seconds an hour. The bound is held at a ceiling (`tests/remaining-balance.test.mjs`) so it cannot widen unseen. Forbidding managers is also the better test of hand play than any single row of the pair table, which is why the comparison is quoted whole.

**The strongest hand pair.** The median hides a spread: the ratio of the day-90 net worth without managers to the net worth with them depends heavily on the pair. Measured on commit 31ddccf with the Quick bot (specialty Operations, seeds 7, 11, 23 and 31; the bot of `tests/bot.mjs` with `hirePipeline` shadowed to refuse, as `balance({ managers: false })` does): Tradesperson with Seasoned Pro ends 2.1 to 12.1 times richer with hiring forbidden at a decision every 4 hours (7.6x, 3.5x, 2.1x and 12.1x), 3.6 to 10.5 times at every 2 hours (8.1x, 10.5x, 3.6x and 10.4x), and slightly poorer at every 8 hours (0.77x to 0.80x on three seeds, 2.1x on one); Legacy Heir with Seasoned Pro is 0.9x to 1.6x at every 4 hours and Well-Connected with Side-Hustler 0.7x to 3.0x. So the design's "the same bot with hiring forbidden must not end richer" is met by the median of the pairs and by most pairs at 4 and 8 hours, and is **not** met by the pair whose strengths are all hand play (+2 Ops, repairs half price, wrecks a fifth cheaper, the free counterfeit read): for them a repaired crate turns over several times a day, the lots of districts 10 to 16 are worth hundreds of thousands, and a manager's 6 to 8% a cycle is a poor use of the capital it ties up. That is intended: the managers are the passive route and skilled hand play is what those Backgrounds pay for, and it is the same reason that "Skill play must matter" (docs/V2_DESIGN.md section 11). It is bounded, not closed: the levers that would close it are the managers' margin and cycle (`PIPE_RULES.marginBase`, `ECON.cycleBase`), which the Codex's worked examples pin, and a lower return on repairs in districts 10 to 16, which the same examples price; `tests/remaining-repair.test.mjs` holds the pair's ratio between 1.5 and 14 at a decision every 4 hours (it was 2.1 to 12.1), so a change that makes hand play dominate more, or less, fails a test instead of drifting.
At one decision a day (`node tests/balance.mjs 5 24`) nobody gets far, and no bound of docs/V2_DESIGN.md section 11 is claimed for it (they are for a decision every 2 to 4 hours, the pace of a class; at 8 hours only the median net worth, district 16 and the looser spread of 4.5 are held): districts 5 / 9 / 13 on days 34.5 / 87.5 / 91 (0d66995: 43.5 / 89 / 91, 816e7c4: 40 / 87 / 91), median net worth 29,000 (26,774 and 27,538), with a spread of 8.7x and five pairs outside 35%, all of them with Digital Native or Seasoned Pro.

### The cadence
The late-game bounds are for a decision every two, three or four hours. At a decision every three hours the whole table above gives 1 run of 330 in district 16 and a spread of 2.5; the 11-pair, three-seed sample that `tests/audit-repair.test.mjs` plays gives 0 of 33 and 3.1 (0d66995: 7 of 33, 21%, and 3.0); every two hours the whole table gives 3% and 2.3 times (that sample 1 of 33 and 2.2; 0d66995: 8 of 33, 24%, and 2.8). On 0d66995 the one-in-six bound held only at every 4 hours (6% on the whole table) and the whole table's spread was 3.2 every two hours. `tests/audit-repair.test.mjs` holds the every-three-hours numbers at a ceiling and `tests/remaining-balance.test.mjs` holds the every-two-hours ones and the day-90 net worth near commit 816e7c4's at every 2, 4 and 8 hours. The bot plays Quick and never opens the Deal Room, so strengths that only pay through skill (Pro's inspection accuracy, Second-Chance's probe) are valued at what they save a Quick player (the free counterfeit warning is heeded by the bot) and not at their skilled-play value: a human who inspects gets more from them. Treat the harness as a floor on the weakest pairs, not a ceiling on the strongest. The first balance pass changed: Well-Connected buyers arrive 30% faster (not 40%) with 15% bigger budgets and an 8% weekly family tax; Grad's garnish is 5% up to $3,000; Immigrant's international lanes gain 10 points (not 15); Second-Chance's bank gate is $8,000; Heir learns 10% slower.

## Districts
Sixteen districts unlock by net worth: $0, 20, 80, 250, 1,000 (Metro Hub: managers, clients and the Bank), 2,500, 6,000, 12,000, 25,000 (Industrial Park: audits), 50,000, 90,000, 150,000, 250,000 (Corporate Valley: enterprise, M&A, IPO), 500,000, 1,000,000, 2,500,000. Return per cycle is deliberately similar everywhere; what grows is the stake, and each district has one signature rule (haggling stalls, semester waves, counterfeits and auctions, estate auctions, repair bays, customs, bulk and compliance, fads, perishables and seasons, royalties, enterprise clients, credit, rent, arbitrage). Opening a district also pays a permanent bonus (`BONUS_EXPECT` in `js/data/schema.js`).

## Hidden truth
A lot is posted "as described" at 97% of its fair value at Ops 1 (`lotDiscHi`), 2.5 points less for each Ops level above 1, never under 84% (`lotDiscLo`); a broken lot is posted far below a working one, at 1.00 to 2.47 times its own as-is value (Formulas, Broken lots). Its real condition (a multiplier of 0.7-1.15, 0.85-1.15 in the first two districts) and whether it is counterfeit (the district's `fakes` rule is the share of its lots that are fake, shared out by each good's `fakeRisk` against the district's mean, so a risky good carries more of it; a fake is worth 8%) are hidden. A lot that is broken is never also fake, and an asset with no `fakeRisk` counts as 1. Net worth counts described value until the truth is known (a buyer's refusal, a pool sale, or reading it off an inspection). Buyers always check what they buy, so a fake is never sold at full price: information is worth money, which is what the Inspect game teaches.
What the rules add up to, measured on thousands of generated lots (`tests/audit-content.test.mjs` holds the same numbers): in the Pawn District 22% of the lots are fakes (about one in five), a watch (full `fakeRisk`) is fake 32% of the time at the gate (the district's rate is shared out over the kinds of goods on its counter, and that list widens with net worth: measured with $10M it is 36%), and 35% of the stock goes to auction; Estate Sales puts about 55% of its lots to auction; six in ten of Auto Row's lots arrive broken (about three in four of the ones that can be repaired); a quarter of Real Estate Row's lots are foreclosure auctions. A Sealed Mystery Box is worth 85% to 115% of what it looks like and is never fake, like any lot in the first two districts.
Two doors would let the truth out for nothing, and are shut. A swap refused on a crate's hidden worth says only that the crate cannot be traded at that price, and it costs 3% of the value it was refused at (`SWAPS.appraisal`, a ledger row of type `verify` and a count in `s.meta.appraisals`): asking for nothing, crate after crate, would be a free counterfeit detector that makes Read pointless. And the first move at a Close or a Bid in the Deal Room is final (`s.tried`): sat at before, the talk is played at Quick, so a negotiation cannot be left, read and played again with its answers in hand.

## Economy knobs (`ECON`)
Values in `js/config.js` today, each with what it does (the same table is the comment above `ECON`):
- `lotDiscHi` 0.97 and `lotDiscLo` 0.84: a sound lot is posted at 97% of its fair value at Ops 1, 2.5 points less for each Ops level above 1, never under 84%.
- `brokenLo` 1 and `brokenSlope` -0.21: a broken lot is posted at `max(brokenLo, 1 - brokenSlope x (Ops - 1))` of its as-is value. The slope is negative on purpose: sellers of wrecks ask for the repair upside, so a better repairer pays more (Formulas, Broken lots). It was 0.9 and 0.015, a wreck at 0.985 down to 0.9 of its as-is value (Ops 1 to 8), which made repairing and reselling worth +200% a cycle at every stake.
- `lotSlots` 4 and `lotEvery` 1.1: lots open at once in a district, and how much slower than the district's own `lotEvery` hours they come (it was 1.5).
- `leadEvery` 0.8: the same for buyers, 0.8 times the district's own interval (it was 1.15), so buyers come 44% more often.
- `urgentK` 3 and `urgentFade` 1: an urgent buyer's budget is fair value x (1 + (0.4 to 2.6) x `urgentK` x (1 - `urgentFade` x how far past district 4, over eight districts)): need pays well at the Scrap Yard, less each district further, and nothing extra from district 12 (it was 0.9 and 0.9, with a tenth of the premium left there). This and the two flows above carry the first two weeks now that wrecks are dearer.
- `cycleBase` 132: a manager's cycle in hours at speed 1 (60 hours in Metro Hub for a Broker). `pitchK` 0.92: buyers pay 8% under the base pitch price. `margin` 1: a manager's return is set by `PIPE_RULES.marginBase`.
- `pointXp` 120: what a skill point is worth in XP once every skill is at mastery. `warnMiss` 0.1: the share of counterfeits the Second-Chance street sense misses.
Also: `PIPE_RULES.marginBase` and `marginSlope` (`marginBase` 1.15, `marginSlope` 0: one margin for every district, held by `tests/audit-engine-core.test.mjs`), `scaleExp` 0.62 (capital and hire cost scale with `(gate / 1000)^scaleExp`) and `overheadExp` 0.5 (overhead with `(gate / 1000)^overheadExp`). A manager's cycle is `cycleBase` / district speed times its own factor (Broker 1.0, Logistics Agent 0.8, Supply-Chain Student 1.3): 60, 48 and 78 hours in Metro Hub. (`PIPE_RULES.cycleBase` of 48 is a v1 leftover that nothing reads.)

How they were chosen. The text of the Codex pins `pitchK`, `margin`, `marginBase`, `cycleBase` and the lot discounts (its worked examples are written for them), and `marginSlope` is held at 0, so those did not move. A longer cycle keeps the return per cycle and takes the return per day down everywhere (`cycleBase` is 132 and was 80 on commit 816e7c4, which also had a margin of 0.4 x (1 - 0.045 x (district - 5)); the run-away an earlier note said the longer cycle cured does not reproduce on 816e7c4, where 3% (every 4 hours) to 9% (every 2) of the runs reached district 16). `scaleExp` (0.4 to 1.0) and `overheadExp` (0 to 0.6) were swept with the day-90 net worth flat beyond noise, and a district's margin cannot come down without a level-1 Broker losing money (tests/audit-engine-core.test.mjs holds it), so the managers' side was left. The tries below were on a 22-pair, 3-seed sample at a decision every 4 hours, on the economy of commit 0d66995 (days to districts 5 / 9 / 13, median net worth): `urgentK` 1.3, 16.5 / 28 / 45, 1.84M; `lotEvery` 1.0, 16.5 / 30 / 45, 1.54M; `lotSlots` 6, 17 / 29 / 47, 1.69M; `lotSlots` 2, 22 / 38 / 57.5, 1.13M (the early game is limited by the lots on offer, the late game is not); `leadEvery` 0.7, 17.5 / 29.5 / 47.5, 1.59M. With a flat price for wrecks (1.7 to 2.3 times the as-is value for everyone) the late game came down as far as needed, but the Backgrounds with no Ops edge (Grad, Heir) fell behind and the spread of net worth went to 4 to 10 times; a price that grows with Ops takes the repair edge out of the Ops skill and out of the Tradesperson's +2, and that is the one kept. `node tests/tune.mjs '{"urgentK":2,"pipe":{"scaleExp":0.7}}' trades,pro 4` prints the days to districts 5, 9, 13 and 16 and the net worth over three seeds for any setting (add `--no-managers`); judge a change over several seeds and several cadences, and change one knob at a time: per-seed variation is large (a first week can swing 10 days).

Open, and for the owners of the files named. The Codex's repair example and the Academy's Fix It lesson price the wreck at its as-is value, which is the price at Ops 1 and not the open market's at Ops 4 (1.63 times). The Draft screen's "buyer flow" trait (`js/ui/draft.js`) does not count `leadBudget` for Grad. The Deal Room phrase table in `js/play/pitch.js` still uses the British spelling of "neighbor".

## Decisions the spec left open (please review)
1. **Balanced Creativity value.** The spec's formula gives 2.36 for `V_niche=10, V_total=100` while its edge-case table says 1.68. The formula and its floor and ceiling (0.40, 3.14) are implemented; the buyer baseline is 0.60 of fair value so a "balanced" niche is worth about 1.0x, and managers normalize by 1.68.
2. **Interest rates** of 2.5% to 8.5% are per 30 virtual days, since a 90-day game would barely register an annual rate.
3. **Urgent leads.** A $0.05 paperclip cannot reach a $3 pen through cost-based pricing, so some leads are urgent: the buyer's stated budget is the reference value. The opening lead is guaranteed.
4. **Cohort volumes** come from real peers plus a deterministic ghost herd. The herd climbs the districts together as a moving bell (center at district `1 + 14 x (day/90)^0.9`) and drifts to a second-best niche every ten days.
5. **Exit.** M&A and IPO value the business at 6x or 10x trailing average daily profit plus book value (times Global Exchange's exit bonus), less 12% brokerage, settling after 48 hours. IPO needs a clean audit record.
6. **Algorithmic events** (viral mention, supply glut, regulatory scare) derive from the room code and day, so every student in a room sees the same ones.
7. **Skill points replace cash training.** Levels (`floor(sqrt(xp / LEVEL_XP)) + 1`, `LEVEL_XP` 150) grant one point each; skills top out at level 8, so a founder can spend 19 points at most (the Heir, with no specialty, 21). The points authored in content stay inside that: quests 8, the Academy 5 and six skill-game epics 1 each, 19 in all; the other epics pay experience only. The engine banks a point only if a skill can still take it (`Game.grantPoints`) and turns the rest into XP.
8. **Per-district rules the data authors added** beyond the required ones: small `fakes` rules in several mid and late districts so `fakeRisk` and Inspect matter there; `gray` markets in the Pawn District and the Global Exchange; `customs` at the Exchange so arbitrage has to beat freight.
9. **Assets that pay must out-earn their wear.** A held asset loses `dep` x 0.2% of its value a day (doubled for digital goods in the Tech Corridor, the fads district), so every asset with a `yield` pays at least 0.15 points a day more than that in the worst case (0.35% to 0.6% a day against wear of 0.2% to 0.4%), and holding any of them for 30 days raises net worth. Real Estate Row pays 25% more rent on top (`rentMult`). Its quest asks for $25,000 of income, which two apartment blocks bought on the day the district opens collect in about five days.
10. **Quick is par, and skill is rare.** On the sell side Quick closes at or below the pitch's fair price, never above; on the buy side it pays 1.0x to 1.18x the asking price, never under. So the rare haggle goals ask for what only skilled play reaches (a sale 10% or more above fair, a purchase 5% or more below the asking price), and the daily "Buy Low" asks for the asking price or lower, which Quick manages about half the time.
